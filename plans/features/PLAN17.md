# PLAN — Phase 17 (Why did our net worth change? External-flow classification, a period decomposition of the net-worth delta, and a waterfall that says whether you earned it or the market did)

> Phase 16 ([PLAN16.md](PLAN16.md)) added the **calendar** dimension — what lands, when, and what that leaves. Round 11 ([IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md)) asks something the app has never been able to answer about the number it puts at the top of the dashboard: ***"We're up $40k this year. Did we earn that, or did the market?"***
>
> The net-worth chart goes up and the household cannot tell whether it reflects their discipline or the S&P. Those two facts imply completely different next actions — one says "keep doing what you're doing", the other says "you got lucky and your savings rate is still wrong" — and the app reports neither.
>
> The deliverable is one waterfall and one sentence: ***"Your net worth rose $40,200. You saved $23,100 of it, the market added $14,800, you paid down $2,300 of principal, and $0 is unexplained."***
>
> **This is the cheapest round in [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) and the one with the highest value-per-line.** It is deterministic accounting over `account_snapshots`, `transactions`, `investment_transactions`, `holding_snapshots`, and `liabilities` — every one of which the app already captures nightly. [investments/performance.ts](../../src/lib/investments/performance.ts) already has `attribution()` (contribution vs. market growth) but only for the *brokerage*; this phase lifts the same idea to the whole household. **There is no new table, no new migration, no new worker job, and no new dependency in this phase.** If a task in the batches below appears to need one, that is the signal to stop and re-read ADR-039 §3.
>
> Phase 17 lands in eight batches: (0) **scope gate + ADRs**; (1) **the household boundary** — external-flow classification and transfer pairing, pure and TDD; (2) **the five explained rungs** — pure, one module per rung; (3) **the decomposition assembler + the identity invariant** — the sharpest correctness surface in the phase; (4) **residual diagnostics** — making `unexplained` name its own causes; (5) **the loader + delivery surfaces**; (6) **tests, privacy pass, docs, E2E**; (7) **the credit-card sign convention** — the one write-path defect this phase's own verification uncovered, closed at the boundary while there is still no card data to migrate.
>
> **Hard boundary ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Domain Scope) — read before writing code.** This phase **explains a change that already happened**. It does **NOT** add: **double-entry accounting or a general ledger** (§Domain Scope names this explicitly — a decomposition is a *report over balances*, and the moment a rung needs a posting rule to be correct, the design is wrong, not the ledger missing); **tax math of any kind** — no realized/unrealized gain split for tax, no cost-basis-for-1099, no wash sales (§Domain Scope, reaffirming ADR-021 and ADR-019); **capital-gains reporting beyond the simple display gain/loss already shipped**; **budgeting or envelopes** (ADR-018 unamended — a rung is a *description of the past*, never a target); **any AI/LLM** over financial data (§Privacy hard-no — every rung is arithmetic with a stated formula); **probabilistic or forward-looking attribution** (ADR-022 stands — this looks strictly backwards); **benchmark-relative attribution, factor models, or Brinson-style sector attribution** (that is portfolio analytics for a professional, not a household question, and it needs data the app does not hold). Lifting net-worth attribution into scope is justified exactly as ADR-018 lifted budgets, ADR-030 lifted income, ADR-032 lifted guidance, and ADR-036 lifted timing: it is the **owner's explicit ask** in [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 11, recorded in [FEATURE.md](FEATURE.md) under P17-00.
>
> **Privacy ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Privacy & Data Sovereignty) — binding, and like Phases 15 and 16, the egress budget is literally zero.** Nothing under `src/lib/net-worth/` makes or transitively imports an outbound HTTP call on any code path. There is no benchmark-index fetch (the `^GSPC` closes this phase might want are already cached in `prices` by the nightly job — ADR-021 — and this phase adds **no** new symbol and no new fetch), no peer-comparison service, no "households like yours" lookup, and no enrichment of any kind. **This phase creates no new PII**: it stores nothing at all, and every figure it computes is a function of rows that already exist. The net-worth delta, the rung amounts, and the residual **never** appear in the content-free notification email (ADR-015) — this phase adds no alert, so the question does not arise, and P17-00 records that as a decision rather than an oversight. No balance, rung, or residual is ever logged. P17-27's privacy regression test enforces all of this.
>
> **Money & Numeric Precision ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Money — CRITICAL).** A waterfall is a chain of signed additions whose *whole purpose* is that it reconciles exactly. A single float rounding in any rung produces a nonzero `unexplained` that is a **lie about the data quality** — the worst possible failure mode for a surface whose residual is meant to be a trust signal. Every value is a `numeric` string combined only through [Money](../../src/lib/money/money.ts)/`decimal.js`. **Never** native `+ - * /`. `Number()` appears exactly once per figure, inside the Recharts island, at the display edge. The residual is computed as `Money.sub` against the anchored delta and is expected to be **exactly** `"0.00"` on a fully-explained fixture — that is an assertion, not an aspiration (P17-12).
>
> **Date discipline.** All windows are UTC-midnight, inclusive `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, resolved through the shipped [analytics/range.ts](../../src/lib/analytics/range.ts) `resolveRange` — the same range toggle `/analysis` already offers, per Round 11's own wording. A range endpoint is an **as-of instant**, and the balance at that instant is "the latest snapshot on or before it" — the rule `computeNetWorth` already implements. This phase does not invent a second as-of rule.

## What already exists (do not rebuild — extend these)

Inventory before writing code. Phase 17's genuinely new surface is **three pure modules and one page**; everything else is composition.

- **The anchor** — [net-worth/compute.ts](../../src/lib/net-worth/compute.ts): `computeNetWorth(asOf, baseCurrency)` returns `{ asOf, total, items[] }` where each item is `{ accountId, accountName, accountType, currency, balance }`. **This is the phase's foundation.** Two calls — one at the range start, one at the range end — give both the authoritative endpoints *and* a per-account balance at each, which is exactly the input every rung needs. Its agreement with `netWorthSeries` (and therefore with the dashboard chart) is already proven by [series-parity.test.ts](../../src/lib/net-worth/series-parity.test.ts). **Do not write a third net-worth valuation path.**
- **Investment attribution** — [investments/performance.ts](../../src/lib/investments/performance.ts): `attribution(startValue, endValue, flows, currency)` → `{ startValue, endValue, netFlows, marketGrowth }`, and `externalFlow(txn)` which classifies an `investment_transactions` row as a boundary-crossing flow (buy/deposit in, sell/withdrawal out; dividend/interest/fee are *internal* and return `null`). Both pure, both `Money`-based, both already tested. **The household investment rung is these two functions applied across every `investment`/`crypto_ira` account.**
- **Spend & income aggregation** — [analytics/spending.ts](../../src/lib/analytics/spending.ts): `spendByCategory`, `spendByMonth`, `spendingSummary`. `spendingSummary` already returns the **uncategorized count and outflow** for a window — that is P17-16's first residual diagnostic, free. The `kind='transfer'` exclusion and the `magnitude()` sign convention are settled here; do not restate them.
- **Range resolution** — [analytics/range.ts](../../src/lib/analytics/range.ts): `resolveRange({range, from, to})` → `{key, from, to, label}` with `3m | 6m | 12m | ytd | custom`. Round 11 says "any range the existing range toggle offers"; this is that toggle.
- **Amortization** — [mortgage/amortization.ts](../../src/lib/mortgage/amortization.ts): `balanceAtMonth(principal, annualRate, termMonths, monthsElapsed)`. Principal paid over a window is `balanceAtMonth(T0) − balanceAtMonth(T1)` — closed-form, exact, no transaction data required. `computeNetWorth` already contains the `monthsBetween` helper this needs; **extract it rather than copying it.**
- **Payroll-direct contributions** — [income-contributions.ts](../../src/db/schema/income-contributions.ts) (`pay_date`, `gross_amount`, `employee_amount`, `employer_amount`) and their materialized twin in [investment-transactions.ts](../../src/db/schema/investment-transactions.ts) with **`source='income'`** (ADR-030/031, Phase 14). This is the money that enters the household and never touches checking, and it is the single largest systematic hole a naive decomposition leaves — see ADR-041 §3.
- **Per-position history** — [holding-snapshots.ts](../../src/db/schema/holding-snapshots.ts): `(holding_id, date)` with `quantity`, `value`, `cost_basis`. Used in this phase **only** for the P17-17 quantity-drift diagnostic, not for valuation — see ADR-039 §2.

- **The loader precedent** — [advice/snapshot.ts](../../src/lib/advice/snapshot.ts) and [cashflow/loader.ts](../../src/lib/cashflow/loader.ts): one `server-only` assembly function per layer, everything downstream pure. Phase 17 gets exactly one.
- **Chart islands** — [analysis/spend-by-month-chart.tsx](../../src/app/analysis/spend-by-month-chart.tsx) and [analysis/category-breakdown-chart.tsx](../../src/app/analysis/category-breakdown-chart.tsx): the shipped `'use client'` Recharts island shape, with `Number()` at the display edge and [chart-colors.ts](../../src/app/analysis/chart-colors.ts) for the palette. Recharts is **3.8.1** and pinned (ADR-005).
- **Nav** — [nav/manifest.ts](../../src/lib/nav/manifest.ts): the Planning section already carries Overview / Budgets / Review queue / Guidance / Cash flow / Recurring bills. One item is added there, not hand-wired (ADR-035).

### ADR-039 — Net-worth attribution as a read-time decomposition anchored to the shipped net-worth value, with `unexplained` as a first-class reported rung

**Status**: ✅ Locked 2026-08-29

**Context**: The dashboard shows a net-worth line and a range delta. A household cannot act on that delta without knowing what caused it. Three questions follow: what is the authoritative total the decomposition must reconcile to, where do the causes come from, and what happens when the causes do not add up to the total.

**Decision**:

1. **The decomposition is anchored to `computeNetWorth`, not to a parallel valuation.** Opening and closing net worth come from `computeNetWorth(T0)` and `computeNetWorth(T1)` — the same function whose agreement with the dashboard chart is already proven by `series-parity.test.ts`. A waterfall whose endpoints disagree with the chart directly above it is worse than no waterfall: it makes the app contradict itself, and the user has no way to know which number to believe. **The endpoints are given; the rungs are the hypothesis.**
2. **The known historical-quantity wart is inherited deliberately, surfaced, and not silently fixed here.** [PLAN_NETWORTH_SERIES.md](PLAN_NETWORTH_SERIES.md) records that historical valuation prices *today's* holding quantities. `holding_snapshots` holds the true historical quantity and could fix it — but doing so changes **displayed history**, which needs its own ADR and its own phase rather than a ride-along in an attribution feature. Phase 17 therefore anchors to the (slightly wrong) shipped number for consistency, **detects** when the wart is in play for a given window (P17-17: a holding whose quantity at T0 differs from its quantity now), and **names it as a residual contributor**. Making an existing inaccuracy visible is progress; changing history as a side effect of a new chart is not.
3. **Nothing is stored, and no job is added.** The decomposition is a pure function of rows that already exist, exactly like ADR-020's unstored yield, ADR-031's derived savings rate, ADR-032 §3's unstored recommendations, and ADR-036 §4's unstored projection. A stored decomposition goes stale the moment a transaction is categorized. **This phase adds zero tables, zero migrations, and zero repeatable jobs** — and that is a design constraint, not an accident of scope.
4. **`unexplained` is a reported rung, not an error.** It is `ΔNW − Σ(explained rungs)`, it is rendered on the waterfall with the same weight as every other rung, and it is **never hidden, clamped, folded into "other", or absorbed into the nearest rung**. A large residual is the app telling the truth about its own inputs, and it is the most valuable output this phase produces (see consequence 3).
5. **Every rung carries its `InputUsed[]`.** Same contract as ADR-032 §2 and ADR-038 §8: a household that thinks a rung is wrong can see the figure, see where it came from, and follow an `href` to fix the input.
6. **The surface is a dedicated route, not an addition to `/analysis`.** [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 11 says "on `/analysis`". `/analysis` already carries a summary strip, a donut, a stacked monthly bar with a forecast month, and a controls row; a waterfall plus its summary sentence plus a rung table would make it the densest page in the app. Phase 17 ships `/analysis/networth` under Planning in the nav manifest, and puts **one sentence plus a link** on `/analysis` where Round 11 expected to find it. This is a refinement of the round, recorded here rather than made silently.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The app answers "did we earn it or did the market" from data captured nightly since Phase 1, with no new schema and no new egress.
- ✅ The waterfall cannot silently disagree with the dashboard, because it starts and ends at the dashboard's own number.
- ✅ Every rung is auditable and every input is linked.
- ⚠️ **The residual is the feature.** A household with uncategorized transactions, a manual-balance account, or unpaired transfers will see a large `unexplained` on day one. That is correct, and the UI must present it as *"here is what the app cannot see"* with named causes and fix links (Batch 4) — never as an apology and never as a defect to be tuned away. This is the input-health signal [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 16 will generalize, and P17-18 emits it in a shape Round 16 can consume without rework.
- ⚠️ Read-time cost: roughly two `computeNetWorth` calls plus five aggregate queries per page load. `computeNetWorth` is a per-account loop and is the expensive half. Measure it (P17-25) before optimizing; if a specific range is slow, the fix is a set-based endpoint query, not a stored table.
- ⚠️ Attribution quality is bounded by categorization quality, exactly as ADR-032 consequence 1 bounded advice quality by input quality. The difference is that here the bound is **quantified and displayed**, which is strictly better than a rule silently degrading.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Compute rungs first and derive closing net worth by summing them | Produces a closing figure that disagrees with the dashboard, with no residual to explain the gap. Inverts which number is trustworthy. |
| Value investment endpoints from `holding_snapshots` (historically correct) instead of the shipped path | More accurate in isolation, but the waterfall would no longer reconcile to the chart, and it changes displayed history through the back door. Needs its own ADR (recorded as an Open Item). |
| Store a nightly `networth_attribution` table | Stale within hours of any categorization edit, and the range is user-chosen — a stored monthly grain cannot answer "year to date" or a custom window. Recomputation is a handful of queries. |
| Hide or clamp `unexplained` below a threshold | Destroys the single most useful output. A residual the user cannot see is a data problem the user cannot fix. |
| Suppress the waterfall until the residual is small | The households that most need to see their data is thin are exactly the ones who would never see the feature. Degrade with a named cause instead (P17-22). |
| Add attribution alerts ("your unexplained share grew") | Round 11 does not ask for it, and a residual alert is really an *input-health* alert — which is Round 16's job, done once, properly, across all inputs. Recorded as an Open Item. |

### ADR-040 — The household boundary is a pairing question, not a category question: `categories.kind` classifies intent, transfer pairing establishes whether money actually left

**Status**: ✅ Locked 2026-08-29

**Context**: `categories.kind ∈ {income, expense, transfer}` (ADR-018) already keeps card payments and internal moves out of spend totals, and Round 11 says the boundary "is already excluded by `categories.kind`; this makes the household boundary explicit." Working it through, `kind` alone is not sufficient, and the gap is exactly where a decomposition goes wrong.

**Decision**:

1. **`kind` states intent; it does not establish that money crossed the boundary.** `kind='transfer'` covers three materially different things: a move between two accounts the app tracks (genuinely internal — nets to zero in net worth), a move to an account the app does **not** track (genuinely external — net worth really fell), and a person-to-person payment (external). Treating all three as internal understates outflows; treating all three as external double-counts every card payment. Neither is acceptable.
2. **A transfer is internal iff its counterparty leg is found in another tracked account.** `pairTransfers` matches a `kind='transfer'` row against candidate rows in *other* accounts with the opposite sign, equal magnitude within a tolerance, inside a ±N-day window — structurally the same problem [cashflow/match.ts](../../src/lib/cashflow/match.ts) already solved for occurrences, and the implementation follows its shape (deterministic pick, one-claim-per-row, documented tie-break).
3. **An unpaired transfer is never guessed.** It is not silently promoted to an outflow and not silently kept internal. It is excluded from `saved` and **reported by volume as a named residual contributor** (P17-16). Guessing here would move real money between two rungs on the strength of a heuristic, which is precisely the class of mistake ADR-036 §2's preview-and-confirm posture exists to prevent.
4. **`excluded_from_baseline` is deliberately NOT applied in this phase, and that divergence from ADR-034 §1 is intentional.** A "Reimbursements" category is excluded from *take-home* because it is not recurring income you can plan against. But a reimbursement is real money that really arrived and really moved net worth, so excluding it here would manufacture a residual. Two questions, two answers, and the divergence is stated in code (P17-03 header) and in `docs/ATTRIBUTION.md` so a future reader does not "fix" one to match the other.
5. **Uncategorized transactions are excluded from every rung and reported by volume.** Identical posture to `spendingSummary`. `NULL` is not `0.00` — the app says how much it could not classify rather than assuming the classification.
6. **The classifier is pure and I/O-free.** It takes rows and returns a verdict. Every DB read lives in the one loader (P17-19).

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The household boundary becomes an explicit, testable predicate rather than an assumption buried in a `ne(kind, 'transfer')` clause.
- ✅ Pairing is reusable: "which transfers are internal" is a question [analytics/spending.ts](../../src/lib/analytics/spending.ts) and a future tax-packet report would both want, and there is now one answer.
- ⚠️ Pairing has a false-negative mode: two legs whose amounts differ (a wire fee taken out in transit) or whose dates straddle the window will not pair, inflating the unpaired-transfer diagnostic. Tolerances are **stated constants with a documented rationale**, not tuned until the residual looks good — tuning a tolerance to shrink a residual is fitting the measurement to the answer.
- ⚠️ A household that tracks only one account will have every transfer unpaired. The diagnostic must say *"transfers to accounts this app does not track"*, not *"unexplained"*, or the user learns nothing from it.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Treat all `kind='transfer'` rows as internal (the naive reading of Round 11) | Money moved to an untracked account genuinely reduces net worth. Silently misattributes it to `unexplained` with no way to find out why. |
| Ask the user to tag each transfer's counterparty account | A schema change and a data-entry burden for a question the app can answer structurally most of the time. Revisit only if pairing recall proves poor in practice. |
| Infer the counterparty from payee text ("XFER TO SAVINGS") | String matching on descriptors is exactly the brittle guess ADR-036 §2 avoided for obligations. Amount-and-date pairing is checkable; a substring is not. |
| Apply `excluded_from_baseline` here for consistency with take-home | Manufactures a residual out of real money. Consistency between two different questions is not a virtue. |

### ADR-041 — The decomposition identity: five explained rungs, full debt payments counted as outflows with principal added back, and payroll-direct contributions re-added exactly as `attribution()` removed them

**Status**: ✅ Locked 2026-08-29

**Context**: This is Phase 17's ADR-038 §2 — the one place where a plausible-looking design silently produces a wrong number. Two traps are structural rather than incidental, and both must be settled before a line of engine code exists.

**Decision** — the identity is:

```text
closingNetWorth − openingNetWorth
  = saved
  + retirementContributions
  + debtPrincipalPaid
  + investmentReturn
  + assetRevaluation
  + unexplained
```

1. **`saved` = external inflows − external outflows over the window, from `transactions`**, using ADR-040's boundary: `kind='income'` rows are inflows, `kind='expense'` rows are outflows, paired transfers are internal (excluded), unpaired transfers are excluded and reported, uncategorized rows are excluded and reported. It is a **cash-flow figure**, and it can be negative.
2. **Debt principal is counted twice, with opposite signs, on purpose — and this is the phase's central trap.** A $2,000 mortgage payment of which $500 is principal appears in `transactions` as a single $2,000 expense, because the app has no principal/interest split on a transaction. `saved` therefore falls by the full $2,000. `debtPrincipalPaid` adds back $500 from the amortization schedule, which is exact and needs no transaction at all. The two net to −$1,500 — the interest — which is precisely the amount by which paying a mortgage reduces net worth. **The alternative framing (excluding principal from outflows and showing no principal rung) is equally correct arithmetically but tells the family nothing**, and Round 11 explicitly asks for the principal rung. The chosen framing is the one a household reads correctly: *"you spent it, and $500 of it you still own."* Mis-implementing this in either direction moves $500 between two rungs while the total still reconciles — which is why P17-13 asserts the rungs individually, not just their sum.
3. **Payroll-direct retirement contributions are their own rung, scoped to `investment_transactions.source='income'`.** A 401k contribution never touches checking, so `transactions` cannot see it and `saved` misses it entirely; meanwhile `attribution()` correctly subtracts it as an external flow so it is not counted as market growth. Left alone it is a large, systematic, recurring hole that lands in `unexplained` every single period. Scoping the rung to `source='income'` — the exact marker Phase 14 writes (ADR-030/031) — guarantees it re-adds **precisely** the flows `attribution()` removed, so the two cancel by construction rather than by coincidence. A brokerage deposit funded from a tracked checking account carries `source='plaid'|'manual'`, is correctly *not* re-added, and reconciles through `saved` and Δcash instead.
4. **`investmentReturn` = Σ over every `investment`/`crypto_ira` account of `attribution(startValue, endValue, flows).marketGrowth`**, where endpoints come from the ADR-039 §1 anchor (the `items[]` breakdown of the two `computeNetWorth` calls) and flows come from `externalFlow()` over `investment_transactions` in the window. Dividends and interest are *internal* by `externalFlow`'s existing rule and therefore land inside `marketGrowth` — correct for a household question ("the portfolio produced this"), and the same convention Phase 9 already ships.
5. **`assetRevaluation` = Δ balance over `property` and `personal_asset` accounts.** These accounts change value only by revaluation — that is what the type *means* — so the delta is the rung, by definition. A capital improvement paid from a tracked account is a real limitation: it shows as spending in `saved` and as revaluation here, which double-counts the household's position. It is documented, not modeled — modeling it needs an improvement-vs-market split the app does not hold and a family cannot supply reliably.
6. **`unexplained` is computed last, as `ΔNW − Σ(the five)`, and never by adjusting a rung.** No rung is ever "corrected" to shrink the residual.
7. **Credit-card sign convention is verified, not assumed.** `account_snapshots.balance` for a `credit_card` account must be negative for the shipped net-worth math to be right. P17-08 asserts this against real data before any rung depends on it; if it does not hold, the finding is recorded and the rung reads the sign explicitly rather than the phase quietly inheriting a bug.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The identity is exact on well-formed data: a fully-categorized, fully-paired fixture reconciles to `unexplained = "0.00"` **exactly**, and P17-12 asserts that literal string.
- ✅ Both structural holes (principal, payroll-direct contributions) are closed by construction rather than by fudge factors.
- ✅ Every rung has a single stated formula that fits in a sentence, which is what makes `docs/ATTRIBUTION.md` writable and the surface defensible.
- ⚠️ Known, documented limitations that land in `unexplained` rather than being modeled: capital improvements to property; a manual-balance account with no transaction feed; a loan with no `liabilities` row (which `computeNetWorth` skips entirely — consistent, but worth naming); the ADR-039 §2 quantity wart; and any window whose endpoints fall on days with no snapshot for some account.
- ⚠️ The interest portion of debt payments is never shown as its own rung. It is inside `saved`, where a household reads it as what it is — money spent. Splitting it out is a plausible future refinement and is recorded as an Open Item, not built.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Exclude principal from outflows and show no principal rung (arithmetically equivalent) | Correct and useless. Round 11 asks the family question "did I pay down debt", and the answer must be a visible rung. |
| Show principal as a non-additive memo line beside the waterfall | A waterfall that does not add up is not a waterfall. Readers check the arithmetic, and they should be able to. |
| Derive `saved` from `Δcash + Δcredit + contributions + principal` (the balance-sheet path) | Reconciles to exactly zero residual by construction — which sounds appealing and is actually the failure. It would mean the app can never detect that its categorization is wrong, destroying the entire trust signal. `saved` must come from the *independent* flow path so the two can disagree. |
| Split interest out of debt payments using the amortization schedule | Doable, but it moves money out of `saved` into a sixth rung and makes `saved` no longer a cash-flow figure the household recognizes. Open Item. |
| Model capital improvements as a rung | Needs data the app does not hold and cannot reliably ask for. Document the limitation instead. |

    - **`/transactions?uncategorized=1` does not exist, and a test now enforces that it can't happen again.** The plan named that route illustratively; the shipped surface for the job is `/analysis/review` (the review queue, already in the nav manifest under Planning). A fix link to a 404 is worse than no link — it tells a household the app has somewhere for them to go and then does not — so P17-18's honesty pass resolves every `href` to an actual App Router `page.tsx`, following one dynamic segment, and fails if the file is missing. The four shipped routes are `/analysis/review`, `/accounts`, `/accounts/[id]` and `/investments`.
    - **The code set is closed in [types.ts](../../src/lib/net-worth/types.ts), and `ResidualDiagnostic.code` was tightened from `string` to it.** P17-18 asks for a closed set so Round 16's registry can enumerate the causes; leaving it `string` would let a future contributor add a code the UI has no copy for. The honesty pass also asserts **every member is reachable** — a declared cause the app never actually emits is worse than not claiming it.
    - **The snapshot-gap threshold is a finding, not a tuning knob.** `compute-snapshots-daily` writes a row **every night**, carrying the previous balance forward for `depository`/`credit_card`/`property`, so on a running install the newest snapshot on or before any date *is* that date. A gap therefore does not mean "the household forgot to update a balance" — it means **the worker has not been running**, which on a self-hosted box usually means the machine was off or the queue is wedged. `STALE_SNAPSHOT_DAYS = 45` (a statement cycle plus two weeks) and the copy says so. Loans are excluded because they never read a snapshot at all; investment accounts because they are valued from holdings × prices and only *fall back* to one.
    - **Three magnitudes are deliberately not the obvious figure.**
      - `snapshot_gap` reports the larger endpoint **balance**, not the account's delta: when both endpoints read the same stale row the delta is exactly `"0.00"`, which would sort a two-month-old house valuation last precisely when it matters most.
      - `untracked_loan` reports `"0.00"`, and that is the honest number rather than a placeholder — `computeNetWorth` skips such a loan at *both* endpoints, so it moves neither the delta nor the residual. What it breaks is net worth itself, which the detail states outright. It sorts last among the nonzero entries and that is the correct ranking of its contribution *to this page*.
      - `quantity_drift` scales the recorded opening **value** by the share-count ratio instead of deriving a unit price, because a per-unit price rounded to cents is `0.00` for most coins and the diagnostic would silently report nothing for exactly the holdings whose quantity moves most. A position with no opening record is counted and named but contributes **no** magnitude: `holding_snapshots` began in Phase 9 with a best-effort backfill, so an absent row means the share count is *unknown*, not zero.
    - **`manual_balance_accounts` is scoped to `depository` and `credit_card`, and the narrow set is the whole point.** A property moving with no transactions is an appraisal that `asset_revaluation` already explains; an investment account's movement is `investment_return`; a loan's is amortization. Only cash and cards have their movement attributed to transaction flow, so only they can move *without* transactions and land the difference straight in the residual. The wide version would have told a household that its home appraisal is a data problem.
    - **`unmatched_investment_deposits` needed a field no rung needs, or it would have flagged every 401(k) contribution.** ADR-041 §3 makes payroll-direct contributions their own rung *because* they have no matching cash leg — the money never passed through a tracked checking account. Without `source` on the diagnostic's investment-transaction shape, the healthiest possible book would have produced a diagnostic on every contribution. `source='income'` is exempt and a test pins it.
    - **Mutation sweep: 18 mutations, and the one survivor was a test passing for the wrong reason.** Deleting the same-account guard (which stops a brokerage's own withdrawal from excusing its own deposit) left the suite green — because the fixture's cash leg sat nine days from the deposit and was already being rejected by the **settlement-window** check. The guard was fine; the test was measuring something else. Re-dated to one day out, it kills the mutation, and the sweep is **18/18**. Four cases were added before the sweep to close gaps the plan exposed: a closing-side-only snapshot gap, a cash leg that settled outside the window, a zero opening share count (the division guard), and a magnitude tie that declaration order alone cannot produce.
    - `pnpm check` green: `tsc --noEmit` clean over 561 files, Biome clean apart from the one pre-existing nursery `useSortedClasses` warning on `account-admin.tsx:219` that PLAN16 already records as unrelated. Full suite **157 files / 1908 tests**; `diagnostics.test.ts` is **42 tests**.

### Batch 7 — The credit-card sign convention, enforced at the write boundary

> **Read this framing first.** Every other batch in Phase 17 is read-only by construction (ADR-039 §3). This one is **not**: it changes what the app writes. It is here anyway because P17-08's ADR-041 §7 verification — a task whose whole purpose was to check an assumption before a rung depended on it — found the assumption **unenforced on two of the three write paths**, and the cost of fixing it rises permanently the moment a credit card exists on the book. Landing it last keeps it out of the decomposition commits; landing it *in this phase* is what makes it free.
>
> **The finding, in full, so a cold session does not have to re-derive it** (established 2026-08-29 under P17-08; see [credit-card-sign.test.ts](../../src/lib/net-worth/credit-card-sign.test.ts) and the [asset-revaluation.ts](../../src/lib/net-worth/asset-revaluation.ts) header):
>
> `account_snapshots.balance` is a **net-worth contribution** — assets positive, liabilities negative — because [compute.ts](../../src/lib/net-worth/compute.ts) sums snapshot balances verbatim and negates **only** `loan` accounts. That convention has never been written down anywhere, which is exactly why the write paths drifted from it:
>
> | Write path | Enforces the card sign? |
> |---|---|
> | Plaid sync — [sync-plaid-item.ts](../../src/worker/jobs/sync-plaid-item.ts), the `ours.type === "credit_card" ? m.negate() : m` line | ✅ negates explicitly |
> | Loan terms — `recordLoanTermsAction` in [accounts/[id]/actions.ts](../../src/app/accounts/[id]/actions.ts) | ✅ writes `-principal` explicitly |
> | Property / personal asset — `recordPropertyValueAction`, `recordAssetValueAction` | ✅ positive by definition |
> | **Manual balance entry** — `recordBalanceAction` (`actions.ts:345`) | ❌ stores verbatim |
> | **QFX/OFX import** — `statementEndingBalance` via [importers/persist.ts](../../src/lib/importers/persist.ts) | ❌ verbatim from the file |
>
> `recordBalanceSchema` ([schemas/account.ts](../../src/lib/schemas/account.ts)) is `moneyAmountSchema`, whose regex makes the leading minus **optional**; [balance-form.tsx](../../src/app/accounts/[id]/balance-form.tsx) labels the field `Balance` with placeholder `1234.56` and offers no hint. A household reading `$1,234.56` off a Visa statement types exactly that, and net worth is overstated by **twice** the balance with no warning anywhere.
>
> Two amplifiers make this worse than a single wrong row. `computeRunningBalances` ([importers/reconcile.ts](../../src/lib/importers/reconcile.ts)) anchors an entire reconstructed balance chain off the prior snapshot or the statement ending balance with **no account-type awareness**, so one sign-flipped statement corrupts every date it touches. And [compute-snapshots-daily.ts](../../src/worker/jobs/compute-snapshots-daily.ts) carries the most recent snapshot forward nightly for `depository`/`credit_card`/`property`, so a single bad row propagates forward indefinitely.
>
> **Why now rather than a later phase.** As of 2026-08-29 the owner's database holds **one account** (a manual `investment`) and **zero** `account_snapshots` rows, so there is nothing to migrate and the fix is a boundary change with no data step. Once a card carries manual or imported history, the same fix becomes a migration *plus* a per-row judgement about which historical values were wrong — and CLAUDE.md §Backup Discipline is explicit that the historical data is the entire product. This is also the phase whose central claim is that `unexplained` is a trust signal; shipping that claim while a known silent 2× error can sit inside the anchor is the wrong order to do things in. (It would surface as a large residual rather than hide — the failure mode working as designed — but "the app can detect it" is a poor substitute for "the app does not write it".)

#### ADR-042 — proposed at plan time, **not yet locked**

**Status**: ⬜ Proposed 2026-08-30 — **P17-30 is the gate.** Append to [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md) §11 Decision Log as **ADR-042** (verify **ADR-041** is the max at commit time — Batch 0 left the log at 41 rows running ADR-001 → ADR-041).

**Context**: The storage convention for `account_snapshots.balance` is real, load-bearing, and undocumented. Three write paths honour it deliberately; two honour it only by luck of what the user typed. The question is not whether to enforce it but *where*, and with what user-visible behaviour.

**Proposed decision**:

1. **State the convention once, in code and in docs.** `account_snapshots.balance` is a net-worth contribution: assets positive, liabilities (`credit_card`, `loan`) negative. It is currently an oral tradition; write it down in [docs/ATTRIBUTION.md](../../docs/ATTRIBUTION.md) (P17-28) and on the helper below.
2. **The manual form speaks "amount owed"; storage speaks net worth.** For a `credit_card` account the field is labelled **Amount owed** and a positive entry is stored **negated** — precisely what the Plaid path already does with Plaid's `balances.current`, which is also an amount owed. An overpaid card (a real, if rare, state) is entered as a *negative* amount owed and stored positive, so the mapping is a plain negation and stays invertible.
3. **The label change is what makes the behaviour change safe, and is therefore not cosmetic.** After this batch, typing `1200` into a card's balance field means something different from what it means today. Nobody has learned the old behaviour — there are no card snapshots — but a future reader must not treat the relabel as optional polish that can be dropped from the diff.
4. **One implementation of the sign rule.** A single pure helper owns it and the **Plaid path is refactored onto it**. Two copies of this rule is precisely how the current drift happened; same argument as P17-07's `monthsBetween` consolidation (ADR-036 §5).
5. **The QFX path validates; it does not normalize.** The OFX `LEDGERBAL` sign for a card is institution-dependent, so an automatic flip would produce a confident wrong answer for some institutions. Instead the import **preview** names the suspected mismatch, shows what will be stored, and requires the existing confirm click — CLAUDE.md §Importers' "parse → preview → confirm, never silently mutate the DB from a file upload", applied literally.
6. **No migration, and that is verified rather than assumed.** P17-30 re-runs the count at commit time. If a `credit_card` snapshot exists by then, the batch grows an explicit correction step with a `pg_dump` first (§Backup Discipline) and that fact is recorded in this plan — it is not silently skipped.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ A household can type what its statement says and get the right net worth.
- ✅ The convention becomes checkable rather than oral, and lives in one function.
- ⚠️ Phase 17 acquires a write-path change, which its own ADR-039 §3 posture excludes. Recorded here as a deliberate, scoped exception with a stated reason, not as scope creep.
- ⚠️ Entering an overpaid card requires a negative "amount owed", which reads oddly. Accepted: it is rare, it is expressible, and the alternative designs make it *inexpressible*.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Reject a positive card balance with a validation error | Hostile to the common case, and it makes an overpaid card unenterable — a real state the app must not forbid. |
| Normalize on write with no label change | The user types `1200` and finds `-1200` later with no explanation. A silent rewrite of user input is not something this app does. |
| Fix the UI only — a hint, no normalization | Relies on the user reading the hint, and leaves the QFX path entirely unguarded. |
| Normalize the OFX ending balance by account type | The sign is institution-dependent; guessing is confidently wrong for some banks. Preview-and-confirm is the shipped posture for imported data. |
| Add a CHECK constraint forcing `credit_card` balances non-positive | Correct-sounding and wrong: it forbids the overpaid card, and a constraint violation surfacing from a nightly carry-forward job is a terrible error path. |
| Defer the whole thing to a later phase | Free today (no card, no snapshots, no migration). After a card has history it is a migration plus a per-row judgement call. The cost only ever rises. |

## Acceptance criteria

Phase 17 is done when all of the following hold:

1. `/analysis/networth` renders a waterfall from opening to closing net worth for any range the existing toggle offers, and its endpoints **equal** the dashboard's net worth for the same dates (P17-26 asserts it).
2. A fully-categorized, fully-paired fixture reconciles with `unexplained === "0.00"` **exactly**, as a string (P17-12).
3. The identity invariant test demonstrably **catches** a deliberately double-counted decomposition (P17-12c).
4. A mortgage-payment fixture asserts `saved` and `debt_principal` **individually**, not merely their sum (P17-13).
5. A payroll-direct 401k contribution moves the `retirement` rung and leaves `unexplained` at zero (P17-13).
6. Every rung carries its `InputUsed[]`, and every residual diagnostic names a specific input and links to where to fix it (P17-18).
7. The privacy regression test passes: zero outbound HTTP under `src/lib/net-worth/`, nothing logged, no new alert type (P17-27).
8. No new table, no new migration, no new repeatable job, no new dependency (ADR-039 §3 — verify at review).
9. `docs/ATTRIBUTION.md` exists and explains the double-signed principal treatment in terms a future maintainer will not "fix" into a bug (P17-28).
10. The E2E proves both halves: a clean book reconciles, and a dirtied book grows its residual **and** surfaces the diagnostic that explains it (P17-29).
11. A manual balance of `1200.00` entered against a `credit_card` account stores `-1200.00` and reduces net worth, through the single sign helper the Plaid path also calls (P17-31/32).
12. A QFX import of a card statement whose ending balance is positive **warns in the preview and stores nothing until confirmed** — it is never auto-flipped (P17-33).
13. Typecheck, `biome ci .`, and the full Vitest suite are green; `pnpm build` is green (or fails only with the known Windows-standalone `EPERM`, which is environmental).

## Open Items (deferred — record, don't build)

Recorded per [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §When in Doubt rather than quietly built or quietly dropped.

1. **Fixing the historical-quantity wart.** Valuing historical points from `holding_snapshots.quantity` instead of current `holdings.quantity` is correct and is now *visible* (P17-17), but it **changes displayed history** and therefore needs its own ADR and its own phase. Phase 17 anchors to the shipped number and names the discrepancy (ADR-039 §2).
2. **Splitting interest out of `saved` as a sixth rung.** The amortization schedule makes it computable. It would make `saved` no longer a cash-flow figure a household recognizes, so it is a real trade-off rather than an obvious improvement (ADR-041 rejected alternatives).
3. **Modeling capital improvements to property.** Needs an improvement-vs-market split the app does not hold. Documented as a limitation instead (ADR-041 §5).
4. **An attribution rung on the dashboard.** Deliberately not shipped (P17-20 skills note). Revisit only if the page proves to be one a household visits often enough that a tile earns its space.
5. **A residual-health alert.** Really an input-health alert, which is [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) **Round 16**'s job, done once across all inputs. P17-18 emits diagnostics in a shape Round 16 can consume; Round 16 owns the alerting (ADR-039 rejected alternatives).
6. **Asking the user to tag a transfer's counterparty account.** Only if pairing recall proves poor in practice; it is a schema change and a data-entry burden for a question structure usually answers (ADR-040 rejected alternatives).
7. **Reusing `pairTransfers` inside [analytics/spending.ts](../../src/lib/analytics/spending.ts).** Spend totals currently drop all `kind='transfer'` rows, which slightly understates outflows for the untracked-counterparty case. Correct, cheap, and out of scope for a phase that must not change existing displayed numbers as a side effect.
8. **Multi-currency attribution.** Every rung assumes the single base currency `computeNetWorth` already assumes. When `fx_rates` plumbing lands, FX revaluation becomes its own rung — and until then a non-base-currency account is excluded upstream, consistently with the shipped total.
