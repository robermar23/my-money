# PLAN — Phase 19 (The second operator: the legal, disclosure, portability and release gaps that stand between a tool that works for one household and software a second homelab can deploy, trust and maintain)

> Every phase before this one added a **capability**. This one adds none. Phase 19 is the first phase whose subject is not the household's money but **the project itself**, and the question it answers is the one nobody has asked in nineteen phases: ***"Could someone other than the author actually run this?"***
>
> **The diagnosis came from an adoption audit on 2026-10-04**, run against the working tree at `717090a` under the `self-host-adoption-audit` discipline: every finding names a file, a symbol or a command, because a speculative gap and a verified one read identically and the second operator cannot tell them apart. The audit's verdict is worth stating in full, because it sets this phase's scope: **the expensive-to-retrofit things are already right.** Money precision (`numeric(18,2)` end to end, one `Money`/`decimal.js` wrapper, `ROUND_HALF_EVEN`), egress discipline (seven `privacy-egress.test.ts` suites walking real module graphs and failing closed on an unlisted import), idempotency (FITID dedup, `/transactions/sync` cursor persistence, `ON CONFLICT` snapshot upserts), and the reconciliation honesty that makes `unexplained` a trust signal rather than a cosmetic zero. **What is missing is the packaging layer.** That is the whole phase: no money number moves, and the architecture is not touched.
>
> **The deliverable is not a feature. It is a state**: a homelab enthusiast who has never spoken to the author can clone or pull, understand what they are legally permitted to do, deploy without a prebuilt binary from a stranger's registry, discover that their backups are not configured *before* they need them, learn in one sentence that the guidance engine assumes US retirement accounts, and know what changed between two versions.
>
> Phase 19 lands in nine batches: (0) **scope gate + ADRs, and the one decision only the owner can make** — the license; (1) **legal and provenance** — `LICENSE`, `SECURITY.md`, and build-your-own parity with the published image; (2) **instance self-disclosure** — the app alarms about *its own* unconfigured safety features, which is the one gap on the list that can cost a second operator everything; (3) ~~the currency trapdoor~~ **scrapped by the owner at P19-00: my-money is USD-only, and no currency work ships** (ADR-051); (4) **release discipline** — CI, version, `CHANGELOG`, and a migration policy; (5) **time** — there is no `TZ` anywhere, so "nightly" is UTC and a Sydney household's nightly jobs run mid-afternoon; (6) **portability labeling** — the US-specific and institution-shaped assumptions declared where they are felt, not generalized away; (7) **onboarding** — demo data, doc accuracy, the papercuts that cost week one; (8) **verification, privacy pass, docs**.
>
> ---
>
> **Hard boundary #1 — "a second operator" does NOT mean "a second user". Read this before writing a line.** The phrase *"another homelab enthusiast might run into gaps"* is one short step from "support two households", and that step is forbidden. [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Domain Scope names **multi-user, household sharing, permissions, RBAC, family/team modes** as explicitly out, and **ADR-004 stands unamended**: one password field, no email, no username, `users` has exactly one row. Phase 19 makes the app **adoptable**, not **multi-tenant**. Concretely it does **NOT** add: a second `users` row, a `user_id` foreign key on anything, roles, invitations, per-user scoping, an admin surface, OAuth or any external identity provider, or a "sign up" path. **The test**: if a task makes the app able to serve two people, it is the wrong task. `make db-reset` + re-bootstrap is still the forgotten-password story, and a second homelab gets its **own instance**, not an account on yours.
>
> **Hard boundary #2 — no telemetry, and this is the phase where someone would add it.** An adoption phase is the single most likely place in this project's history for "anonymous install statistics", "opt-in crash reporting", a Sentry DSN, a version-check ping, or a "help us improve" dialog to arrive wearing a reasonable argument. [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Privacy forbids every one of them, and the project **exists because commercial alternatives sell user data** — shipping a phone-home in the name of making the app easier to adopt would defeat the thing being adopted. Specifically out: **no analytics SaaS, no telemetry of any kind, no error reporting to a vendor, no update-check HTTP call, no CDN-hosted user data, no marketing pixel.** This phase's net egress change is **zero**, and P19-36 asserts it over the whole tree rather than over one new directory.
>
> **Hard boundary #3 — no figure moves.** This is ADR-048 §2's *"the projection is UNCHANGED"* discipline applied at phase scale, and it is the invariant that makes a packaging phase safe to merge. **Every money figure, chart series, rung, recommendation, requirement and projection the app produces must be byte-identical before and after Phase 19**, with exactly one disclosed exception, which replaces *silence* rather than a number: **new alert rows** can appear on an instance whose backups are unconfigured (ADR-052) — new rows in `alerts`, no change to any existing one. *(A second exception, ADR-051's currency refusal, was scrapped at P19-00 along with the rest of the currency work.)* P19-31 asserts this as a parity run, not as a claim.
>
> **Hard boundary #4 — nothing is generalized speculatively.** The audit found institution-shaped enums (`crypto_ira`, `troy_oz`) and one bank's CSV preset. The temptation is to generalize the data model for households that do not exist yet. **Do not.** ADR-053 §3 makes the decision explicit: the author's enums were **correct for the author**, and the finding is that the shape is **nowhere labeled**, not that it is wrong. A `grams` quantity unit is one migration whenever a real household asks; a speculative taxonomy rewrite is a schema change against the data CLAUDE.md §Backup Discipline calls the entire product. **Label, do not refactor.**
>

> **Privacy ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Privacy & Data Sovereignty) — binding, and unusually load-bearing this phase.** Beyond Hard boundary #2, three specifics. (1) **Demo/seed data (P19-32) must be synthetic and must refuse to run against a non-empty database** — a seeder that can overwrite a real book is a data-loss bug wearing a convenience label, and it is the one new destructive capability this phase contemplates. (2) **The instance-health surface (P19-10) reports configuration state, never financial state** — "backups: not configured" is a fact about the deployment; "backups: last covered $847,000 of net worth" is a figure, and it does not belong on a page whose whole job is to be safe to screenshot into a support thread. (3) **`CONTRIBUTING.md` and `SECURITY.md` are the first documents in the repo addressed to a stranger**, so they must not invite one to paste a `.env`, a `pg_dump`, a Plaid token or a transaction row into an issue; the vulnerability-reporting path is private by construction.

The currency boundary work originally planned here was scrapped at P19-00 (ADR-051): my-money is USD-only by the owner's decision, and `Money.assertSameCurrency` ([money.ts:207](../../src/lib/money/money.ts)) stays exactly as it is.

## What already exists (do not rebuild — extend these)

Inventory before writing code. Phase 19's genuinely new surface is **four documents, one alert rule, one settings page, one CI workflow and one seeder**; everything else is a label, a constant, or a correction.

- **The alert machinery is complete and this phase adds one rule to it, not a mechanism.** [alerts/rules.ts](../../src/lib/alerts/rules.ts) carries the `AlertType` union (12 members), `DetectedAlert`, `dedupHash(type, subjectId, periodKey)`, and **three deliberately separate sweep lists** — `RULE_ALERT_TYPES`, `CASHFLOW_ALERT_TYPES`, `CASHFLOW_SWEPT_ALERT_TYPES` — whose header already explains, at length, why adding a type to the wrong list resolves an alert at 05:00 that another job raised at 02:00. **Read that header before touching the union.** The `alerts` table's type CHECK is a `sql` list at [alerts.ts:83](../../src/db/schema/alerts.ts), so a new member is a migration *and* a union edit *and* a sweep-ownership decision.
- **The backup job already knows it is disabled, and already says so to nobody.** [backup-database-daily.ts:153-155](../../src/worker/jobs/backup-database-daily.ts) reads `env.BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT`, and when it is absent emits `console.error("[backup-database-daily] disabled: … skipping")` and returns a result with a `disabled` flag ([:43](../../src/worker/jobs/backup-database-daily.ts)). **The signal exists; it goes to a log nobody tails.** Batch 2 is therefore small — it routes an existing fact to an existing surface, and the `disabled` flag is the hook.
- **`backup_failure` is already written by the job directly** (not by `detect-anomalies-daily`) and is already **excluded from `RULE_ALERT_TYPES`** for that reason, with [alerts/backup-alert.ts](../../src/lib/alerts/backup-alert.ts) owning the write and the clear-on-success. The new type follows that precedent exactly and must be excluded from the same list for the same reason.
- **`fx_rates` is a complete table with no writer.** [db/schema/fx-rates.ts](../../src/db/schema/fx-rates.ts) exists; `accounts.currency` exists and defaults to `'USD'` ([accounts.ts:69](../../src/db/schema/accounts.ts)) with a header that says *"net-worth aggregation converts via `fx_rates` if needed"*; `Money.assertSameCurrency` throws a message naming it. **`grep -rn 'insert(fxRates)' src` returns nothing, and `src/lib/prices/` has Yahoo, CoinGecko, metals and fundamentals fetchers and no FX fetcher.** The table is structurally guaranteed empty. The audit called this its worst finding — a **trapdoor** for a non-USD household. **Scrapped at P19-00 (ADR-051):** the owner runs a USD-only instance and does not want to carry currency work, so the finding is answered with one documented sentence ("USD only"), not code.
- **Statutory limits are year-keyed, `null`-on-unconfigured, and tested.** [advice/constants.ts](../../src/lib/advice/constants.ts) holds `STATUTORY_LIMITS` as a `ReadonlyMap` with **2024, 2025 and 2026 and nothing later**, `limitsForYear()` returning `null` rather than carrying a year forward, and a header that states the maintenance ritual — *"the IRS announces the following year's figures each autumn … Do not 'carry forward' a year"*. **The design is right. The problem is that the instruction lives only in that comment**, so expiry is experienced as the plan page quietly losing rungs.
- **`/api/health` already exists** ([src/app/api/health](../../src/app/api/health)) and is wired as the compose healthcheck via Node's global `fetch` (no curl in the image). Batch 2's instance-health **page** is a different thing — human-readable configuration state, session-guarded — and must not duplicate the probe's contract or make the probe heavier.
- **There is no central env module.** `find src/lib -name 'env*' -o -name '*config*'` returns nothing; env is read at point of use (`env.BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT`, `env.SMTP_URL`, the `ALERT_*` thresholds via `intFromEnv` in [alerts/rules.ts](../../src/lib/alerts/rules.ts)). Batch 2 needs one **read-only, server-only** place that answers "which optional subsystems are configured" — and it must be a *reporter*, not a validator that could refuse to boot a working instance.
- **Settings is a real section with four pages** — `categories`, `connections`, `household`, `income`, plus a `layout.tsx` — and [nav/manifest.ts](../../src/lib/nav/manifest.ts) is the single source for the sidebar, the per-section strip and the ⌘K `page` results (ADR-035). One entry is **added to the manifest**, never hand-wired.
- **The CSV importer already supports presets and headerless files.** [importers/csv/parse.ts](../../src/lib/importers/csv/parse.ts) synthesizes `col_N` headers when `hasHeader: false`, and [docs/IMPORT.md](../../docs/IMPORT.md) documents three presets (generic signed, generic debit/credit, M&T headerless). **Adding a preset is data, not code** — which is exactly why Batch 6 adds presets rather than a mapping DSL.
- **The multi-arch build is done and documented.** [Dockerfile](../../Dockerfile) builds clean for `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64` with every native dep's prebuild named in the header, and `make buildx-init` / `image-build` / `image-push` exist. **The gap is not the capability; it is that [docs/DEPLOY.md](../../docs/DEPLOY.md) routes everyone to `robermar2/my-money` and says "No build toolchain … is needed on the server".** Batch 1 promotes the existing path to co-equal; it builds nothing new.
- **The Makefile is the real operator interface** — 40+ targets including `up`, `down`, `nuke`, `prod-pull`, `prod-up`, `db-migrate`, `db-reset`, `db-reset-password`, `db-reset-plaid`, and a `job-*` target per worker job. A second operator who finds the Makefile is in good shape; the README's "PRD Commands" section, which **hardcodes `/mnt/user/appdata/my-money/repo/`**, is what they find first.
- **Egress discipline** — **seven** shipped `privacy-egress.test.ts` suites ([advice](../../src/lib/advice/privacy-egress.test.ts), cashflow, dashboard, income, net-worth, search, watchlist) that walk real module graphs and fail closed on an unlisted import specifier; P18-18's note records the advice suite **failing closed as designed** when `src/lib/sinking/queries.ts` appeared. This is the mechanism Hard boundary #2 extends tree-wide at P19-36 rather than a thing to rebuild.
- **The three threat models** — [THREAT_MODEL.md](../../docs/THREAT_MODEL.md), [THREAT_MODEL_PLAID.md](../../docs/THREAT_MODEL_PLAID.md), [THREAT_MODEL_MARKETDATA.md](../../docs/THREAT_MODEL_MARKETDATA.md). `SECURITY.md` (P19-03) **summarizes and links these; it authors no fourth one**, because this phase adds no external integration and no outbound surface.
- **Naming hazard.** `health` already means the `/api/health` liveness probe; `setup` is a directory the README documents and **which does not exist**; `status` means an obligation/expense lifecycle field in two schemas. The new page is **`/settings/instance`**, the new module is **`src/lib/instance/`**, and the new alert type is **`backup_not_configured`**. Do not name anything in this phase `health`, `setup`, `telemetry`, `diagnostics`, or `onboarding`.

### ADR-050 — The project is licensed, and the license is the owner's decision, not the plan's

**Status**: proposed — **P19-00 is the gate, and this is the one ADR the owner must decide rather than ratify**. **Decided by the owner 2026-10-10: AGPL-3.0-or-later.**

**Context**: There is no `LICENSE`, no `COPYING`, and no `license` field in [package.json](../../package.json) (`"private": true`, `"version": "0.0.0"`). Under the Berne Convention the work is **all rights reserved by default**: a second homelabber has no legal permission to deploy, modify, or redistribute it, and a cautious one will not. **Every other finding in the adoption audit is downstream of this one** — CI, a changelog, a build-your-own path and a contributing guide are all work in service of adoption that cannot legally happen. It is also the cheapest item on the list by an order of magnitude, which is why it is Batch 1 and not Batch 8.

**Decision**:

1. **A license file is added at the repository root, and `package.json` carries the matching SPDX identifier.** `"private": true` **stays** — it prevents an accidental `npm publish` and says nothing about the license.
2. **The choice is the owner's and P19-00 does not proceed without it.** [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §When in Doubt is explicit — *"if the question isn't covered there, **ask the user** — do not silently pick"* — and a license is irrevocable in practice once a third party has relied on it. The plan therefore states the options and the recommendation and stops.
3. **Recommended: AGPL-3.0-or-later.** The reasoning is the project's own: my-money exists *specifically because* commercial alternatives monetize user data, and the AGPL is the one common license whose network clause prevents a hosted fork from taking the work closed and reintroducing exactly that. It is also what the nearest comparable (Firefly III) uses, so it is unsurprising to this audience.
4. **Alternative, recorded so the choice is informed: MIT or Apache-2.0.** Maximum adoption, zero friction, and the accepted cost that a SaaS could wrap it. Apache-2.0 adds a patent grant and an explicit trademark reservation. If the owner's goal is "the most people possible run this", this is the honest pick and §3's reasoning does not override it.
5. **Also a valid outcome: `LicenseRef-Proprietary` — "source-visible, no grant".** If the owner does not want adopters, that is a legitimate decision, and **stating it is still strictly better than silence**, because silence costs a prospective adopter an afternoon before they reach the same conclusion. **If this is chosen, Batches 1 and 4 shrink to the two items that serve the author alone** (CI, and a changelog) and the rest of the phase is deferred rather than built — recorded here so the plan degrades honestly instead of being half-executed.
6. **Third-party notices are verified, not assumed.** The dependency licenses are enumerated once (`pnpm licenses list`) and any copyleft or attribution-requiring dependency is recorded. This is a check, not a rewrite: the stack is ordinary MIT/Apache TypeScript, and the expected finding is "nothing to do".

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The blocker that gates every other adoption question is removed in one commit, and the project becomes something a stranger can act on.
- ✅ Recording a *refusal* (§5) is also a complete outcome, so the gate cannot deadlock.
- ⚠️ **Practically irrevocable.** A license can be changed going forward, but copies already distributed under the old terms stay licensed under them. This is why it is a gate and not a task.
- ⚠️ AGPL is a real deterrent to some corporate contributors. Accepted: this is a household finance tracker, not a platform play.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Pick a license in the plan and move on | §When in Doubt forbids silently deciding, and this decision is practically irrevocable. A plan may recommend; it may not choose. |
| Leave it unlicensed and address it "if someone asks" | Nobody asks. They read `ls LICENSE*`, get nothing, and leave — and the author never learns it happened. |
| Add a `LICENSE` but keep `"private": true` out of the discussion | They are orthogonal and both correct: the flag guards against publishing, the file grants rights. Conflating them is how one gets dropped. |
| Dual-license (AGPL + commercial) | A business model, not a packaging fix. Nobody has asked to pay for this. |

### ADR-051 — my-money is USD-only, by the owner's decision; no currency work ships

**Status**: **decided by the owner at P19-00, 2026-10-10.** This replaces the plan-time proposal ("a single base currency enforced at the write boundary, `fx_rates` reserved"), which was **scrapped**.

**Context**: The audit found `fx_rates` has no writer, `currency` columns default to `'USD'` on 11 tables, and `Money.assertSameCurrency` throws a message naming a remedy no code can perform — a trapdoor for a household that sets an account to `EUR`. The plan proposed closing it with write-boundary refusals across every form and importer, a base-currency source of truth, Plaid-currency handling, field-level copy and a `docs/CURRENCY.md`. At the gate the owner ruled it out: *"I only want to worry about U.S. currency … way too much for me to worry about."*

**Decision**:

1. **my-money supports US dollars only.** That is stated once in the README and [docs/DEPLOY.md](../../docs/DEPLOY.md) as a plain limitation, alongside ADR-053's other US-specific labels. It is a label, not a feature.
2. **No currency code ships.** No write-boundary refusal, no base-currency module, no change to the household-profile currency field, no Plaid `iso_currency_code` handling, no `docs/CURRENCY.md`, no FX. Batch 3 (P19-14 → P19-17) is removed.
3. **Nothing is touched or dropped.** The `currency` columns, `fx_rates` and `Money.assertSameCurrency` stay exactly as they are (CLAUDE.md §Database — never drop a column without a backup step, and here there is nothing to gain).

**Consequences**:
- ✅ Phase 19 sheds a whole batch and every money-path change it contained; Hard boundary #3 gets simpler (one sanctioned exception, not two).
- ⚠️ The trapdoor stays open for anyone who sets a non-USD currency. Accepted: the owner is USD-only, and the README says so.

### ADR-052 — The instance reports its own operational state: an opt-in safety feature that is inert must say so, through the alert path it already owns

**Status**: proposed — P19-00 is the gate

**Context**: [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Backup Discipline is unambiguous — *"Backups are not optional; the app's value is the historical data"* and *"an untested backup is not a backup"*. The implementation is good: a nightly `pg_dump | age` at 04:00 whose plaintext never touches disk, with the private identity deliberately off-host (ADR-014). **And it is off by default**: [backup-database-daily.ts:153](../../src/worker/jobs/backup-database-daily.ts) returns early with a `disabled` flag when `BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT` is unset, logging to stderr.

Now trace the alarm path. `backup_failure` exists as an `AlertType`, is written directly by the job, and is excluded from `RULE_ALERT_TYPES` so the anomaly sweep cannot resolve it. **But a disabled job never fails.** It skips. So the alert cannot fire, and a second operator can run my-money for a year — accumulating exactly the irreplaceable history the project is *for* — with **zero backups and zero signal**, having read a README that describes the backup system in the present tense. The same shape applies to the Beancount longevity mirror (`BEANCOUNT_DIR` unset ⇒ the job skips) and, less severely, to alert email (`SMTP_URL` unset ⇒ `/alerts` is the only channel, which is a documented and reasonable default).

This is a general class, and it is worth naming once: **an opt-in safety feature whose failure path cannot execute is silent by construction.** The audit's rule of thumb — *absence of a `*_not_configured` alert means absence of signal* — generalizes beyond backups, which is why this ADR is about disclosure rather than about one env var.

**Decision**:

1. **One new alert type, `backup_not_configured`**, written by the backup job on the path where it currently returns early. Severity **`warning`** — not `critical`, because an instance on day one legitimately has no backup configured and a critical alert on first boot trains the operator to ignore the channel; and not `info`, because this is the project's stated core value being unprotected.
2. **It is written by the job and cleared by the job**, following `backup_failure`'s precedent exactly ([alerts/backup-alert.ts](../../src/lib/alerts/backup-alert.ts)): resolved on the first successful backup, and **excluded from `RULE_ALERT_TYPES`** so `detect-anomalies-daily`'s sweep cannot resolve a condition it never detected. The `rules.ts` header already explains why that list is not "the swept types in general" — this is the fourth type to rely on that reasoning, and the sweep-disjointness test covers it.
3. **The dedup key is period-based so it states the condition without nagging**, through the shipped `dedupHash(type, subjectId, periodKey)`. A standing misconfiguration produces one open alert, not one per night — the discipline ADR-048 §4 applied to a configuration fact.
4. **No new alert type for the Beancount mirror, and no new type for SMTP.** Beancount is a *longevity hedge* over data Postgres still holds, so its absence is not a data-loss risk — it belongs on the instance page (§5), not in the alert stream. SMTP-absent is an explicitly documented default (ADR-015: *"leave `SMTP_URL` empty to use `/alerts` as the only channel"`), and alerting about a missing alert transport through that transport is a loop. **Recorded as decisions, not oversights.**
5. **A read-only instance surface at `/settings/instance`** answers, in one screen, what a second operator currently cannot determine without shell access: which optional subsystems are configured (backups, off-host replication reminder, Beancount, SMTP, Plaid env, tunnel), the applied migration count vs. the repo's, the app version and image tag, the worker's liveness (⚠️ **verify what is actually available before promising it** — [heartbeat.ts](../../src/worker/jobs/heartbeat.ts) is a Phase 0 placeholder that `console.log`s and **persists nothing**, so there is no stored timestamp to read; either derive liveness from the most recent job row that does persist, or extend the heartbeat to write one, and record which), the last successful backup timestamp **and the last restore drill the operator recorded**, and **which statutory-limit years are configured** (ADR-053 §2). Session-guarded like every other page.
6. **It reports configuration, never figures.** No balance, no net worth, no account name, no transaction — the page must be safe to screenshot into a public issue, which is precisely what a second operator will do when asking for help. P19-36 asserts this rather than trusting it.
7. **It is a reporter, not a validator.** It must never refuse to boot, never fail a healthcheck, and never block a page render on a subsystem being unconfigured. A working instance with no backups configured is a *warned* instance, not a broken one — the app does not get to hold the household's data hostage to its own advice.
8. **The restore drill becomes recordable, because §Backup Discipline already demands it quarterly.** One nullable timestamp the operator sets after following [docs/RESTORE.md](../../docs/RESTORE.md), surfaced with its age. **No verification is attempted and none is implied** — the app cannot know whether a restore actually worked, and pretending to would be worse than silence. It records *that the human says they did it, and when*, which is the honest and useful thing.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The one gap on the audit list that can cost a second operator **everything** produces a signal through a channel that already exists, in roughly one rule's worth of code.
- ✅ The general class is closed rather than one instance of it: §5's page is where the next opt-in subsystem gets disclosed, so this does not recur per feature.
- ✅ `/settings/instance` is the page a second operator links in a support thread, which makes every future question answerable without asking them to run psql.
- ⚠️ **One new alert row can appear on an existing instance** — the only sanctioned exception to Hard boundary #3, and it appears exactly when the alert is true.
- ⚠️ A new `AlertType` means a **migration** (the type CHECK at [alerts.ts:83](../../src/db/schema/alerts.ts) is a `sql` list), a union edit, and a sweep-list decision. Three coupled edits, and the sweep list is the one that silently misbehaves if it is wrong.
- ⚠️ The recorded restore drill is **self-reported**. Stated on the page in those words, so nobody mistakes it for a verified backup.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Enable backups by default | Requires generating an `age` keypair the app would then hold — and ADR-014's entire security property is that the **private identity never lives on the server**. Auto-enabling would either break that or produce undecryptable dumps. |
| Make the app refuse to start without `BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT` | Holds the household's data hostage to its own advice, and bricks an otherwise working deployment over a warning. §7 forbids it. |
| Reuse `backup_failure` for the unconfigured case | Conflates "your backup broke" with "you never set one up" — different causes, different fixes, different urgency, and the same dedup key would make one mask the other. |
| Put it only on the instance page, no alert | A page nobody visits is not a signal. The alert is what brings them to the page. |
| Add `*_not_configured` for every optional env var | Alert fatigue, and most are genuinely optional with no data-loss consequence. The test is whether inertness risks **irreplaceable data**; only backups pass it. |
| Have the app verify the backup by test-decrypting | Requires the private identity on the server, destroying ADR-014's property. The drill is deliberately human. |

### ADR-053 — Author-shaped assumptions are **labeled where they are felt**, and expiring constant tables surface their own expiry

**Status**: proposed — P19-00 is the gate

**Context**: Nineteen phases of single-household use encoded that household's world in enums, presets and constants. The audit enumerated them; **none is a defect, and the finding in every case is that the shape is nowhere declared.** The structural ones:

| Assumption | Where (verified) | What the second operator experiences |
|---|---|---|
| US retirement system — 401(k)/403(b)/457, IRA, HSA, catch-up at 50/55 | [advice/constants.ts](../../src/lib/advice/constants.ts) `StatutoryLimits` | Several **ranked rungs** of the savings waterfall are meaningless, not merely unused |
| IRS limits for **2024–2026 only** | `STATUTORY_LIMITS` map | Parts of `/plan` go dark in January until a human edits a `Map` and redeploys |
| `^GSPC` as *the* benchmark | investments/performance | A non-US investor is compared to the S&P 500 with no alternative |
| Plaid as the only aggregator | ADR-008 | No GoCardless/Nordigen (what European self-hosters actually use), no SimpleFIN |
| `crypto_ira` as a first-class account type | [db/schema/enums.ts](../../src/db/schema/enums.ts) `accountTypeEnum` | A semantically odd slot; and no `hsa` type despite the guidance engine recommending HSA funding |
| `troy_oz` but no gram/kilo | `quantityUnitEnum` | Most of the non-US world buys bullion in grams |
| One bank's CSV preset (M&T, headerless, index-keyed) | [docs/IMPORT.md](../../docs/IMPORT.md) §Preset 3 | Hand-writing JSON column maps — the single most-cited friction in comparable projects' reviews |

**Decision**:

1. **Label; do not refactor.** The README and the relevant docs state the assumptions in plain sentences — *"the guidance engine at `/plan` implements US retirement accounts (401k/IRA/HSA) and will not produce meaningful rungs outside the US"* — and the affected surfaces say it where they are read. **No enum is widened, no taxonomy is rewritten, and no constant is generalized on speculation** (Hard boundary #4). One honest sentence saves an adopter an afternoon; a speculative schema change spends a migration against the data §Backup Discipline calls the entire product.
2. **The statutory-limits cliff becomes self-disclosing, which is the only behavioural change in this ADR.** `limitsForYear() → null` is correct and tested (ADR-034 §5) and is **not touched**. What changes is that the *maintenance burden stops living only in a code comment*: `configuredLimitYears()` — which the constants module already exposes for exactly this purpose — drives (a) the coverage line on `/settings/instance` (ADR-052 §5), and (b) the existing `unknown_input` copy on the affected rungs, which already suppresses with a reason and now names **which year is missing and which file supplies it**. A second operator reads "limits are not configured for 2027" instead of watching advice vanish.
3. **Institution-shaped enums are documented as author-shaped and left alone.** `enums.ts` headers record that `crypto_ira` exists because the author holds metals in an iTrust IRA, that `quantity_unit` covers the units the author owns, and that **adding a member is one migration whenever a real household asks** — which is cheap, honest, and strictly better than guessing at a taxonomy for households that do not exist. An `hsa` account type is **not** added despite the guidance engine naming HSAs, because `investment` already models it and a new enum member without a real holder is the speculative refactor this boundary forbids. Recorded as an Open Item with its trigger stated.
4. **CSV presets are extended as data, because that is the cheapest high-value portability work available.** [importers/csv/parse.ts](../../src/lib/importers/csv/parse.ts) already handles headerless files and `col_N` indices, so adding presets for common export shapes (Chase, AmEx, Capital One, generic ISO-date, generic European `DD/MM/YYYY` + comma decimal) is **a fixtures-and-docs task with no parser change** — and the European date/decimal shape is the one that is currently silently *wrong* rather than merely absent, so it ships with a test. **No mapping DSL, no auto-detection beyond what exists.**
   > **Corrected at P19-00 (2026-10-10): the "no parser change" premise is false, and the European shape is worse than "silently wrong dates".** [`normalizeAmount`](../../src/lib/importers/csv/parse.ts) strips **every** comma before validating, so `12,50` imports as **`1250`** and `1.234,56` as **`1.23456`**. Both pass the `^-?\d+(\.\d+)?$` check, so a wrong money figure is written with no symptom. `normalizeDate` auto-detects `NN/NN/YYYY` as month-first, so `03/02/2026` imports as March 2, and `CsvColumnMapping.dateFormat` has no day-first member to select. **A European preset cannot be expressed as data.** The decision is narrowed accordingly, in line with Hard boundary #4: **(a)** the importer's US assumptions (month-first dates, `.` decimal, `,` thousands) are **labeled** in [docs/IMPORT.md](../../docs/IMPORT.md) and at the upload form; **(b)** ~~a comma-decimal refusal~~ — dropped with ADR-051: the owner is US-only, so non-US file formats are labeled, not guarded; **(c)** day-first dates and comma-decimal parsing are an **Open Item**; **(d)** the Chase / AmEx / Capital One presets are **deferred**. The `personal-tool-builder` counterweight failed them: the author holds none of those accounts, cannot verify them, and *"don't add if you imagine someone might want"* is that skill's rule. The trigger to revisit is a real export from a real user.
5. **ADR-002's Tailscale path is documented, because it was decided and never written down.** ADR-002 locked *"Cloudflare Tunnel **or** Tailscale — pick at deploy time"* and only the tunnel got docs, so a LAN-only operator reads [docs/PLAID.md](../../docs/PLAID.md) and concludes they need a public domain. **They do not**: webhooks are a latency optimization, and the 03:00 safety-net sync already covers a tunnel-less install. One documented section, no code — and the Plaid page states the degradation rather than implying a requirement.
6. **The Plaid production-approval cliff is disclosed as a prerequisite, not a footnote.** [.env.example](../../.env.example) currently frames Plaid as a pricing question (*"100 free Items then ~$0.30/mo"*). The real friction is that production access requires **per-operator approval that is a genuine coin-flip for an individual**, is non-transferable, and gates the app's flagship ingestion path — with QFX/CSV import, which the README labels "**Fallback**", as the remaining route. Stated in [docs/PLAID.md](../../docs/PLAID.md) and the README before anyone invests a weekend.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ A second operator can determine in one README pass whether this app fits their country, their banks and their accounts — which is the question they actually have.
- ✅ The January cliff stops being a mystery and becomes a one-line maintenance task with a named file.
- ✅ Hard boundary #3 holds: §2 changes *copy and a page*, not a figure; the rungs already suppressed.
- ⚠️ The project is now **explicitly** US-centric in writing. That is a feature of honesty, not a regression — it was always true.
- ⚠️ ~~New CSV presets are untested against real exports from those banks~~. Superseded at P19-00: the unverifiable bank presets are deferred (§4 correction). What ships is a label and one refusal, and neither needs a bank's export to verify.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Generalize the account-type and quantity-unit enums now | A speculative schema change for households that do not exist, against historical financial data. One migration when a real user asks is cheaper and better-informed. |
| Make statutory limits configurable in the DB/UI | A real feature with a real surface, for a maintenance task that is one hand-edited `Map` row per year. The cliff needs *disclosure*, not a settings page. |
| Auto-carry-forward last year's limits | Exactly what ADR-034 §5 forbids, for exactly the reason it gives: silently confident wrong dollars every January, vs. an honest suppression the user can act on. |
| Make the benchmark index configurable | A genuine feature touching a shipped performance surface, and Hard boundary #3 says no figure moves this phase. Open Item. |
| Add a second aggregator (GoCardless/SimpleFIN) | A whole phase: a new vendor, a new threat model, a new token-encryption path, a new sync cursor semantic. Not a label. Open Item. |
| Build a CSV mapping UI / auto-detect dialect | The parser already takes a JSON map and the presets already auto-fill it. A DSL is a feature; presets are data. |

### ADR-054 — A release is identifiable, verifiable and reversible: CI on push, a real version, a changelog, and an explicit migration policy

**Status**: proposed — P19-00 is the gate

**Context**: Four verified facts that compound into one problem. (1) **No CI** — `ls -a .github .gitea .forgejo` finds nothing, against ~2,281 tests in 180 files that the author runs by hand. (2) **No version identity** — `"version": "0.0.0"`, no git tags, no `CHANGELOG.md`, and both compose services pull `robermar2/my-money:${MONEY_IMAGE_TAG:-latest}`. (3) **Migrations are not applied on boot** — 26 of them (`0000`–`0025`; 25 at audit time, ADR-049 added `0025`), with `drizzle-kit migrate` living at the bottom of the README under "PRD Commands" with the author's absolute Unraid paths baked in. (4) **The test suite needs infrastructure** — `TEST_DATABASE_URL` plus a Postgres on 5434, and [vitest.setup.ts](../../vitest.setup.ts) *refuses to run* unless the target database is "test"-named (a good guard, and a cliff for a newcomer who just wants to verify their fork).

Together: a second operator pulls a new `:latest`, the schema and the image disagree, and they have no changelog to consult, no tag to pin, no green check to trust, and no documented rollback. **This is the failure mode that turns an adopter into an ex-adopter**, and the comparable-project reviews the audit surfaced name post-install database trouble as the thing that makes people abandon a finance tracker outright.

**Decision**:

1. **CI runs on push and pull request**, and because the repo is hosted on the owner's **self-hosted Gitea** the workflow targets **Gitea Actions** (`.gitea/workflows/`) with a `.github/workflows/` copy added **only if** the project is also mirrored to GitHub. The job: `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile`, `pnpm check` (Biome + `tsc --noEmit`), `pnpm test` against a **Postgres service container** with `TEST_DATABASE_URL` wired to it, and `pnpm build`. Playwright is **not** in the default job (it needs browsers and a running stack) — it gets a separate manually-triggered workflow, so the fast gate stays fast and the slow one stays runnable.
2. **The version is real and the image is pinned.** `package.json` carries a semver, git tags match, and the image is published under both `:<version>` and `:<git-sha>`. **`:latest` keeps working** — breaking the owner's existing `prod-pull` would be a self-inflicted outage — but [docs/DEPLOY.md](../../docs/DEPLOY.md) recommends pinning `MONEY_IMAGE_TAG` and says why.
3. **`CHANGELOG.md`, Keep-a-Changelog shape, with a mandatory `Migrations` line per release** naming the highest migration that release requires. This is the single most load-bearing line in the document: it is what lets an operator answer *"do I need to migrate, and can I go back?"* without reading a diff. Phases 0–18 are summarized retroactively **from the shipped README's phase entries**, not reconstructed from git archaeology — the README is already the project's de-facto changelog and the PLAN files are the detail.
4. **Migrations stay explicit by default, and the policy is written down.** Auto-migrate-on-boot is **not** made the default: a container that mutates the schema of a financial database on start, unattended, with no backup step, is the opposite of §Backup Discipline, and a failed partial migration during an automatic restart is the worst possible state for this data. Instead: an **opt-in** `MONEY_AUTO_MIGRATE` flag, off by default, documented with its risk stated plainly — **and the recommended path is the existing explicit `drizzle-kit migrate` command, promoted out of the README's footer into [docs/DEPLOY.md](../../docs/DEPLOY.md) with portable paths**. The upgrade ritual (pull → read the changelog's `Migrations` line → **back up** → migrate → restart) becomes a documented sequence with the backup step *inside* it.
5. **A version mismatch is detected and shown, never acted on.** `/settings/instance` (ADR-052 §5) compares migrations applied in the database against those present in the image and reports a mismatch in words — *"the database is at 0023; this image expects 0024. Run `make prod-migrate`."* It **does not** auto-migrate, and it **does not** phone home to check for a newer release (Hard boundary #2 — an update check is an outbound request that leaks that this instance exists). Purely local comparison.
6. **Contributing and security docs exist and set the real bar.** `CONTRIBUTING.md`: the test-infrastructure prerequisite (the 5434 database and why the setup file refuses otherwise), `pnpm check` + `pnpm test` as the gate, Biome as the single formatter, the ADR trail as the place design decisions live, and the standing expectation that [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)'s guardrails are **non-negotiable** rather than style preferences. `SECURITY.md`: a **private** disclosure channel, the single-user threat model, a pointer to the three shipped `THREAT_MODEL*.md` documents, and an explicit *"never paste a `.env`, a dump, a Plaid token, or a transaction row into an issue"*.
7. **The README's absolute paths go.** The "PRD Commands" block hardcodes `/mnt/user/appdata/my-money/repo/`, which is the author's Unraid layout and works nowhere else. These become Makefile targets (or a documented `MONEY_REPO_DIR`), and the author's own operational commands keep working.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ A second operator can fork, run the suite, see a green check, pin a version, read what changed, and upgrade with a documented rollback.
- ✅ The author gets the larger share of the benefit immediately: 2,281 tests stop depending on remembering to run them.
- ✅ Hard boundary #3 holds trivially — nothing in this ADR touches a computation.
- ⚠️ **CI needs a runner.** A self-hosted Gitea needs `act_runner` registered, which is infrastructure work on the homelab rather than in this repo. Called out as a prerequisite at P19-00 rather than discovered at P19-18, and recorded as the one task in this phase that may be **blocked on something outside the tree**.
- ⚠️ A retroactive changelog for 19 phases is a judgement call about granularity. Decided: **one entry per phase**, matching how the README already narrates history, not one per PR.
- ⚠️ `MONEY_AUTO_MIGRATE` is a footgun by construction. It ships off, documented as such, and the instance page reports when it is on.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Auto-migrate on boot by default | Unattended schema mutation of a financial database with no backup step, where a partial failure during a restart loop is the worst state this data can be in. Opt-in with the risk stated. |
| Skip CI; the author runs tests locally | Works for the author and transmits nothing to anyone else. A fork with no green check is unverifiable by its own owner. |
| Put Playwright in the default CI job | Browsers plus a full stack makes the fast gate slow, and a slow gate gets skipped. Separate workflow. |
| Reconstruct the changelog from `git log` | 304 commits of batch-level detail nobody will read, when the README already carries the phase-level narrative that is the right granularity. |
| Add a version-check / update-notification ping | An outbound request that leaks the instance's existence. Hard boundary #2 and §Privacy both forbid it; the local migration comparison gives the useful half for free. |
| Drop `:latest` in favour of pinned tags only | Breaks the owner's shipped `prod-pull` workflow as a side effect of a packaging change. Keep it, recommend against it. |

- **P19-25** — The honest caveat for the day-boundary, which `TZ` does **not** solve. `account_snapshots` keys to **UTC midnight** by design, so for an operator at UTC+10/+13 the snapshot day boundary cuts through their afternoon and a transaction posted at 9pm local may land on the next UTC day. Document it in [docs/DEPLOY.md](../../docs/DEPLOY.md) and `/settings/instance` (one line, with the operator's resolved offset shown so they can judge it). **Do not attempt a local-midnight snapshot boundary this phase** — that is a change to the anchor beneath every historical figure the app has ever computed, it breaks Hard boundary #3 outright, and it is an Open Item with real design content. *Skills*: `cron-doctor` (**the repeatable-job schedule is being re-timed, which is exactly its question** — confirm the existing repeatable jobs' keys and whether changing the evaluation zone re-registers or duplicates them), `bullmq-specialist`.

- **Batch 5 record (2026-10-10).**
    - **P19-24: the plan's fix would have moved figures, so it shipped narrower, and the reason was measured.** The plan said to wire `TZ` so *"BullMQ's repeatable-job cron expressions evaluate in the operator's zone"*: all of them. Reading what each job dates its writes by found the problem. The price fetch stores Yahoo's **latest** price (`regularMarketPrice`) under **the UTC date at the moment it runs** ([yahoo-finance.ts](../../src/lib/prices/yahoo-finance.ts), `todayIso()`). At 01:30 UTC that is the previous US session's close. At 01:30 Sydney (14:30/15:30 UTC) the US market is open, so every stored "close" would be a mid-session price, permanently. From UTC+1:30 eastward, even a post-close run would file prices under a different date than today. **That breaks Hard boundary #3 for exactly the operator P19-24 was for.** So each job now names its clock in [src/worker/schedule.ts](../../src/worker/schedule.ts):
        - **UTC, pinned explicitly:** prices, contributions, snapshots, the Plaid safety-net, anomalies, and the two weekly jobs.
        - **The operator's zone:** only the two jobs that write no figure, the backup (04:00) and the Beancount export (04:30).
        - Patterns are byte-identical to before. A test asserts that `src/worker/index.ts` has no `pattern:` of its own.
    - **The pin also closes a hazard the plan did not name.** No pattern named a zone before, so BullMQ evaluated every cron in the **process's** local time (cron-parser's default, verified: no `tz` → local). It was UTC only because nothing set `TZ`, yet three shipped texts told operators to set it:
        - DEPLOY.md §8: *"set the host/container `TZ` accordingly"*;
        - `unraid.override.yaml`, twice, commented out.
      Following either would have moved the whole data chain. The override's suggestion now points at `MONEY_TZ`.
    - **`MONEY_TZ` in `.env`, passed to the containers as `TZ`.** Compose reads `MONEY_TZ` so that a `TZ` exported in the host's shell cannot leak in through compose interpolation, where the shell wins over `--env-file`. Both `app` and `worker` get it; the worker schedules, and the app only describes.
    - **Robustness:**
        - **Invalid zone.** cron-parser's error for an invalid zone is `CronDate: unhandled timestamp: null`, thrown from a top-level `await` at worker boot, which would stop every job, the backup included. `resolveScheduleZone` validates through `Intl`, the same engine luxon (and so cron-parser) uses. On a bad value the worker logs it and those two jobs stay on UTC.
        - **Empty `TZ` and missing zone data.** In `node:22-bookworm-slim`, an empty `TZ` behaves as UTC, and named zones resolve with no `tzdata` package because Node bundles ICU.
    - **`cron-doctor`'s question, answered by measurement.** A zone change **replaces** a schedule; it never duplicates one. Probed against Redis on a throwaway queue, after no-tz → UTC → Sydney → New York (both remove+upsert and upsert alone), there was 1 scheduler and 1 delayed job each time. Then the real worker was booted three times against the dev Redis:
        - with `TZ=Mars/Olympus`: the warning was logged, everything ran on UTC, and 1 schedule plus 1 pending run per queue;
        - with `TZ=Australia/Sydney`: only the backup and the export moved (next 17:00/17:30 UTC = 04:00/04:30 AEDT);
        - with `TZ` unset: everything back on UTC.
      *Daylight saving:* cron-parser 4.9 slides a skipped time onto the next hour, so New York's spring-forward day ran the `0 2` and `0 3` patterns at the same instant. The operator jobs avoid this: across all 418 zones Node ships, no daylight-saving change in 2026–2027 skips or repeats 04:00 or 04:30 local.
    - **The UTC discipline is now enforced, not just observed.** `TZ` used to be inert; now it moves the process's local clock. So `utc-discipline.test.ts` fails on any local-clock getter or setter, or any multi-argument `Date` constructor, in `src/`, and the tree has none. The calendar-date displays already pass `timeZone: "UTC"`. Server-rendered *timestamps* without a zone (Settings → Connections) will now show in the operator's zone, which is display, not a figure.
    - **One ordering trade-off, documented.** With `MONEY_TZ` set, `detect-anomalies-daily` (05:00 UTC) no longer runs an hour after the backup. A failed backup still writes `backup_failure` immediately, but its email can wait for the next 05:00 UTC run.
    - **P19-25: the day boundary, said with the operator's own numbers.** Settings → Instance gains a **Time** section:
        - the worker's zone and its current offset (the app's own zone if the worker has not reported);
        - the nightly schedule, with the UTC data window converted to local time;
        - *"Days are UTC dates; a new day starts at 11:00 here"*.
      DEPLOY.md §Time zone gives the long form: what changes, what does not, and where your day and the app's diverge. That last part was verified in code: forms default "today" to the UTC date (`toISOString().slice(0, 10)` in the balance, asset and property forms). Typed and bank-supplied dates are stored as given. ALERTS, BACKUP, BEANCOUNT and RESTORE no longer call their times "local".
    - **Found in passing:** the dev database is behind (no `restore_drill_at`; it needs `pnpm db:migrate`). The boot test's worker picked up a queued `evaluate-advice` job, which failed on that missing column. It only reads, so nothing was written.
    - **Verified:** `pnpm check` is clean (the one pre-existing warning); **`pnpm test`: 197 files / 2,467 tests**.
