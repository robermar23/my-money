# PLAN — Phase 16 (The calendar dimension: recurring-obligation detection, a confirmed schedule of what is due when, a deterministic daily cash-flow projection, and a single safe-to-spend number)

> Rounds 2–8 ([IDEAS2.md](IDEAS2.md)) made the app **descriptive**. Round 9 made it **prescriptive** — [PLAN15.md](PLAN15.md) shipped a deterministic rules engine that recommends structures and amounts. Round 10 ([IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md)) asks for something neither of those provides: ***"Can I spend $400 on the car repair this week, or does something bounce?"***
>
> Every number the app produces today is a **stock** (balance, net worth, allocation, cost basis) or a **rate** (savings rate, monthly spend, APR, TWR). Almost nothing is a **date**. A household's money runs on a calendar — the mortgage on the 1st, daycare on the 3rd, paychecks on the 15th and 30th, insurance twice a year, property tax in November. Phase 16 adds the missing dimension: what lands, when, and what that leaves.
>
> The deliverable is one sentence a family acts on: ***"Your lowest projected checking balance is $312 on March 14."*** and one number on the dashboard: **safe-to-spend**.
>
> **This phase is unusually assembly-heavy.** [income/schedule.ts](../../src/lib/income/schedule.ts) already expands a pay cadence into real dates with correct clamping. [advice/net-income.ts](../../src/lib/advice/net-income.ts) already derives observed take-home. [analytics/forecast.ts](../../src/lib/analytics/forecast.ts) already run-rates a partial month. `account_snapshots` already holds the starting balance. Read §What already exists before writing a line — the genuinely new code here is a detector, a projection engine, and one table.
>
> Phase 16 lands in nine batches: (0) **scope gate + ADRs**; (1) **generalized occurrence date math** — one implementation, shared with income; (2) **schema + migration**; (3) **recurring detection** — pure, TDD; (4) **the projection engine** — pure, TDD, and the sharpest correctness surface in the phase; (5) **persistence, matching, and the confirm flow**; (6) **worker jobs + alerts**; (7) **delivery surfaces**; (8) **tests, docs, privacy pass, E2E**.
>
> **Hard boundary ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Domain Scope) — read before writing code.** This phase reports **timing**. It does **NOT** add: **bill pay, money movement, or ACH initiation of any kind** (§Domain Scope names this explicitly — we are a read-only tracker, and a "due date" is an observation, never an instruction); **envelope budgeting** — no money is partitioned, reserved, allocated, or moved between virtual buckets, and Round 2's simple monthly targets stay simple (ADR-018 unamended); **double-entry accounting** (§Domain Scope — obligations are not liabilities and do not post); **any AI/LLM** over financial data (§Privacy hard-no — detection is a deterministic periodicity test with a stated tolerance, and if it cannot decide it says so); **probabilistic cash-flow modeling** (ADR-022's rejection of Monte Carlo applies here identically — the projection is a single deterministic line, never a fan chart); **user-authored goals** (ADR-022, ADR-032 §6 — an obligation is a recorded fact about a recurring cost, not a target the user funds); **live rate, fee, or biller data of any kind** (§Privacy — zero new egress, see below). Lifting cash-flow timing into scope is justified the same way ADR-018 lifted budgets, ADR-030 lifted income, and ADR-032 lifted guidance: it is the **owner's explicit ask** in [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 10, recorded in [FEATURE.md](FEATURE.md) under P16-00.
>
> **Privacy ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Privacy & Data Sovereignty) — binding; no new egress class, and like Phase 15, literally zero.** Nothing in `src/lib/cashflow/` makes an outbound HTTP request on any code path. There is no biller directory, no merchant-enrichment service, no "we recognized this as Netflix" lookup — payee normalization is local string work over the user's own transaction names. Obligation labels are **new user-authored PII-adjacent text** (they name real vendors, landlords, and in practice sometimes people): local Postgres only, riding the nightly `age`-encrypted `pg_dump` (ADR-014). Obligation labels **may** enter the ⌘K index (they are payee text of the kind already indexed via `transactions.name`, ADR-027/028) — but the projection figures, the safe-to-spend number, and the low-water balance **never** appear in the content-free notification email (ADR-015: numbers live in `detail`, and the email carries neither). No balance, projection, or obligation amount is ever logged. P16-00 plus the Batch 8 privacy pass enforce all of this with a regression test.
>
> **Money & Numeric Precision ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Money — CRITICAL).** The projection engine walks day by day accumulating a running balance across a 60-to-365-day horizon. That is **hundreds of sequential additions and subtractions on money values** — the single largest chain of money arithmetic in the codebase, larger than PLAN15's payoff simulator. A float here does not merely round; it drifts monotonically, and it drifts into a number the user makes a spending decision on. Every value is a `numeric` string combined only through [Money](../../src/lib/money/money.ts)/`decimal.js`. **Never** native `+ - * /`. `Number()` appears only at the display edge inside the Recharts island. The **residual discretionary rate** (ADR-038 §2) is a division and must be `Money.div`, never `/`.
>
> **Date discipline.** All date math is UTC-midnight on `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, per the invariant already stated in [income/schedule.ts](../../src/lib/income/schedule.ts): *"A DST transition cannot move a pay date."* A DST transition must not move a due date either. There is exactly one occurrence-expansion implementation after Batch 1 (ADR-036 §5) — two would be two chances to drift.
>

### ADR-036 — Cash-flow timing as a first-class dimension: detect-and-confirm scheduled obligations, never auto-commit; one occurrence-expansion implementation shared with income

**Status**: ✅ Locked 2026-08-23

**Context**: The app models balances and rates but not dates, so the most-asked household money question — *"can I spend this without something bouncing?"* — is unanswerable in principle rather than merely unimplemented. Answering it requires knowing what is due and when. Three questions follow immediately: where does that knowledge come from, how much authority does the app have to write it down, and does a periodicity detector contradict a decision Phase 15 already made?

**Decision**:

1. **Detection is a scheduling tool, not a classification tool — and this does not overturn PLAN15.** [PLAN15.md](PLAN15.md) §Open Items deferred recurring detection because *"`categories.necessity` is explicit and correctable; a merchant-cadence detector is guessing where an answer exists."* That reasoning stands and is **reaffirmed, not reversed**: `necessity` answers *"is this essential?"*, a **classification** question, and detection must never be used to answer it. Round 10 asks *"when does it land, and for how much?"* — a **timing** question on an orthogonal axis that `necessity` cannot answer at any level of classification quality. Detection here schedules; it does not classify. A confirmed obligation inherits its `category_id` from the transactions that produced it and changes no category's `necessity`.
2. **Detection proposes; the user disposes.** The detector emits **candidates** and writes **no** `scheduled_obligations` row. Confirmation is an explicit user action through the shipped review idiom ([analysis/review](../../src/app/analysis/review/)). This is [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Importers' *"never silently mutate the DB from a file upload; show the user what will be added and require a click"* applied to a second inference surface. A detector that silently created rows would put invented obligations into a number the user spends against — the exact failure §Importers exists to prevent.
3. **Manual entry is a first-class path, not a fallback.** The bills that hurt most are often the ones that never touch a tracked account — a premium paid from an untracked account, tuition on a payment plan, a quarterly estimated payment. Manual obligations are indistinguishable downstream from confirmed detected ones; only `source` differs, for provenance.
4. **The projection is computed on read and never stored.** Same principle as ADR-020's unstored yield, ADR-031's derived savings rate, and ADR-032 §3's unstored recommendations: a stored projection is a projection that goes stale silently, and this one goes stale the moment a transaction posts. The **only** persisted state is the obligation record and its occurrence rows (which are facts about a schedule, not derived figures).
5. **Exactly one occurrence-expansion implementation.** `payDatesBetween` is generalized into a shared `occurrenceDatesBetween` and income **delegates to it**; obligations do not get a parallel copy. This is ADR-031 §"one savings rate, one implementation" applied to date math, and the stakes are the same — a second implementation is a second chance for a clamping or DST bug to appear in only one of them.
6. **Safe-to-spend is a derived floor-adjusted low-water mark, not a budget.** It is `min(projected closing balance over the horizon) − cash_floor`, clamped at zero. It allocates nothing, reserves nothing, and moves nothing. It is a *report on the worst point of a projection*, which is why it is compatible with §Domain Scope's exclusion of envelope budgeting.
7. **The projection line is single and deterministic.** No confidence bands, no fan chart, no distribution. ADR-022 rejected probabilistic projection and that applies here identically. Uncertainty is expressed as **stated inputs the user can see and correct** — the same auditability contract as ADR-032 §2 — not as a shaded region that implies a rigor the model does not have.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The app answers a question it structurally could not answer before, using data it already holds.
- ✅ Every projection input is visible and correctable; a wrong safe-to-spend number points at a wrong *obligation*, which the user can fix in one click.
- ✅ Zero egress, zero new dependencies, works fully offline.
- ✅ One date-math implementation, shared and already battle-tested by the income materializer.
- ⚠️ Projection quality is bounded by obligation coverage. A household that confirms nothing gets a projection that is only a run-rate — and it must **say so**, not silently present a thin projection as a complete one (P16-30).
- ⚠️ Detection will miss variable-amount bills and mis-group payees that change their descriptor string. Recall is not the goal; **precision plus an easy manual path** is. A missed bill the user adds by hand is a minor annoyance; an invented bill in a spending decision is a defect.
- ⚠️ Generalizing `payDatesBetween` touches shipped income materialization. Batch 1 is a **pure refactor with the existing tests as the gate** — no behavior change, no new cadence reachable from `income_sources`.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Auto-create obligations from high-confidence detections | Puts inferred rows into a number the user spends against. §Importers' preview→confirm posture exists for exactly this class of mistake, and a bill is higher-stakes than an import row. |
| Skip detection; manual entry only | The data is already there and hand-entering twenty recurring bills is the reason nobody would ever turn this on. Detection is what makes it usable on day one. |
| A hosted biller/merchant-enrichment API to identify recurring vendors | §Privacy hard-no: it ships the user's payee list to a third party. Local string work over the user's own data is sufficient for periodicity. |
| Store the daily projection nightly | Stale within hours of any posted transaction. Recomputation is cheap (a few hundred Decimal ops over an in-memory event list). |
| Model obligations as `liabilities` rows | A liability is a principal balance that amortizes. A $14 subscription is not a liability, and `liabilities` has `NOT NULL` columns (`principal_at_origination`, `term_months`, `start_date`) that are meaningless for one — the same reasoning that produced a separate `credit_lines` in ADR-033 §3. |
| Copy `payDatesBetween` and extend the copy | Two implementations of clamping date math is how a DST or month-length bug ends up fixed in one place and not the other. |
| A probability band around the projection | ADR-022 stands. A band implies calibration we do not have and cannot validate on one household's history. |

### ADR-037 — Schema for the calendar dimension: a `scheduled_obligations` table with materialized `obligation_occurrences`, an obligation cadence vocabulary wider than income's, and two `household_profile` settings

**Status**: ✅ Locked 2026-08-23

**Context**: An obligation needs a schedule, an expected amount, an account, and a provenance trail back to the transactions that suggested it. Occurrences need to be *matched* against reality so the app can say "the mortgage did not post" — and matching needs somewhere to record the match idempotently. Phase 14 solved a structurally identical problem for pay dates and the answer should not be re-litigated.

**Decision** — all additive; no existing `NOT NULL` column is touched:

1. **`scheduled_obligations`** — `id uuid pk`, `account_id uuid` FK → `accounts` (`onDelete: cascade`), `category_id uuid` FK → `categories` (`onDelete: set null`, nullable), `label text not null`, `expected_amount numeric(18,2) not null` (**positive magnitude**, sign carried by `direction`), `direction text not null` CHECK ∈ `('outflow','inflow')`, `currency text not null default 'USD'`, `cadence text not null` CHECK ∈ the ADR-037 §3 vocabulary, `anchor_date date not null`, `start_date date not null`, `end_date date` (nullable = open-ended), `active boolean not null default true`, `amount_mode text not null default 'fixed'` CHECK ∈ `('fixed','trailing_median')`, `source text not null` CHECK ∈ `('detected','manual')`, `detection_key text` (the normalized payee that produced it — nullable for manual), `confidence text` CHECK ∈ `('high','medium','low')` nullable, `notes text`, `created_at`/`updated_at timestamptz`. Non-negativity CHECK on `expected_amount`; a partial UNIQUE index on `(account_id, detection_key)` **where `detection_key is not null`**, so one payee series cannot be confirmed twice while unlimited manual obligations remain legal.
2. **`obligation_occurrences`** — `id uuid pk`, `obligation_id uuid` FK (`onDelete: cascade`), `due_date date not null`, `expected_amount numeric(18,2) not null` (**snapshotted at materialization**, so editing an obligation does not silently rewrite history), `status text not null` CHECK ∈ `('scheduled','matched','missed','skipped')`, `matched_transaction_id uuid` FK → `transactions` (`onDelete: set null`, nullable), `matched_amount numeric(18,2)` nullable, `matched_at timestamptz` nullable, `created_at timestamptz`. **UNIQUE `(obligation_id, due_date)`** — the idempotency key, exactly mirroring `income_contributions`' UNIQUE `(rule, pay_date)` from P14-09. Index on `(due_date, status)` for the projection and the miss sweep.
3. **A wider cadence vocabulary for obligations than for income.** `income_sources.cadence` stays exactly `weekly | biweekly | semimonthly | monthly` — its CHECK is **not** touched and `periodsPerYear` keeps its four-member map. `scheduled_obligations.cadence` is that set **plus** `quarterly | semiannual | annual`. Both are expanded by the same shared function (ADR-036 §5); the two vocabularies are separate `const` tuples with `ObligationCadence` a declared superset of `IncomeCadence`, verified by a compile-time assertion so a future edit to either cannot silently diverge.
4. **Two settings on the existing `household_profile` singleton**, not a new table: `cash_floor numeric(18,2)` (the buffer safe-to-spend subtracts; NULL = 0) and `projection_horizon_days integer` (NULL = the 60-day default), with a sane-range CHECK (14–365).
5. **Occurrences are materialized forward on a schedule, not computed on read.** The materializer walks each active obligation from the last materialized occurrence to `today + horizon` and upserts. This is a deliberate copy of P14-09's design and it buys the same three things: the miss sweep has rows to sweep, a matched occurrence has somewhere to live, and re-running the job is a provable no-op via the UNIQUE key.
6. **Occurrence-to-transaction matching is recorded, never inferred at read time.** A match is a durable fact with a timestamp; recomputing it on every page load would let a match flicker as data changes around it.
7. **Text+CHECK, not `pgEnum`**, for every vocabulary here — consistent with `transactions.source`, `holding_lots.source`, `income_sources.cadence` (ADR-030), and `accounts.subtype` (ADR-033 §2). Extending a CHECK beats an enum-add migration.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ Every Round 10 question becomes computable, and the miss/drift alerts get a durable substrate rather than a recomputed guess.
- ✅ Entirely additive: no `DROP NOT NULL`, no data movement, no risk to shipped income or mortgage code.
- ✅ The occurrence table mirrors a pattern already proven in production by the income materializer, including its idempotency story.
- ⚠️ Two tables that are empty until the user confirms something. Batch 5's confirm flow and Batch 7's empty states exist precisely so the schema does not ship inert.
- ⚠️ Snapshotting `expected_amount` onto each occurrence means editing an obligation does not retroactively change past occurrences. That is the intent (history should not rewrite itself) but it must be stated in the UI, or an amount edit will look like it silently failed.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| No occurrence table; expand dates on read | Then a match, a skip, and a miss have nowhere to live, and "the mortgage did not post" becomes a recomputed inference that flickers. P14-09 already answered this. |
| One cadence vocabulary shared by income and obligations | A quarterly *paycheck* is not a thing this app models, and widening `income_sources.cadence` would put `periodsPerYear` — the savings-rate annualizer — one careless edit away from a wrong denominator on a shipped number. |
| Sign the amount instead of a `direction` column | Sign conventions differ between `transactions.amount` and display, and a signed "expected amount" invites a sign bug in the one place a sign bug is most expensive. An explicit `direction` is unambiguous at every call site. |
| Put `cash_floor` / horizon in a new `cashflow_settings` table | `household_profile` is the established singleton for exactly this class of setting (ADR-033 §4). A second settings table is pure sprawl. |
| Store the matched flag on `transactions` | Pollutes a core table with a Phase-16 concern and inverts the relationship — one occurrence has one transaction, and the occurrence is the thing being tracked. |

### ADR-038 — Projection methodology: obligations plus a residual discretionary rate (never double-counted), inflows from observed take-home phased by the pay schedule, a stated horizon and floor, and honest degradation when coverage is thin

**Status**: ✅ Locked 2026-08-23

**Context**: A daily balance projection is trivial to write and easy to write *wrong* in a way that looks right. The failure is not a crash; it is a plausible number that is quietly too low, which trains the household to ignore it. Four methodology questions decide whether the number is trustworthy: what to do about the fact that obligations are already inside observed spend, what "income" means, how far out to look, and what to show when there is not enough to look at.

**Decision**:

1. **Outflows are the union of confirmed obligation occurrences and a residual discretionary rate.** Obligations are dated and exact. Everything else is spread as a flat daily rate.
2. **The discretionary rate is a RESIDUAL, and this is the phase's defining correctness rule.** Recurring obligations are *already inside* observed spend. Taking a total-spend run-rate **and** adding the mortgage on the 1st counts the mortgage twice, and the resulting safe-to-spend number is badly, silently low. The rate is therefore `(trailing observed expense − expense attributable to confirmed obligations in the same window) ÷ days in window`, computed with `Money.div`. Attribution uses the **occurrence match** (ADR-037 §6) — matched occurrences name the exact transactions to exclude, so the subtraction is exact rather than estimated. Unmatched-but-confirmed obligations in the window are excluded at their occurrence `expected_amount`. **P16-16 is a test-first task and the invariant it asserts is the one that must never regress: for any fixture, `projected total outflow over a full month` must not exceed `observed monthly spend + newly-added obligations`.**
3. **Inflows come from observed take-home phased by the pay schedule, never from gross.** Amount from `observedNetIncome`'s trailing median per period (ADR-034 §1 — the deposits that actually landed *are* take-home); dates from `occurrenceDatesBetween` over the income source's cadence and anchor. `income_sources.gross_per_period` is **not** a projection input — projecting gross into a checking account overstates every inflow by the whole withholding stack. Inflow obligations (ADR-037 §1 `direction='inflow'`) cover the irregular receipts income sources do not model.
4. **The horizon is stated and user-settable; the default is 60 days.** Long enough to contain a full pay cycle plus a monthly bill cycle plus the next one — the window in which a family can still *act*. Configurable 14–365 (ADR-037 §4). The horizon is always displayed with the number; a low-water mark without its window is not a fact.
5. **Safe-to-spend = `min(closing balance across the horizon) − cash_floor`, clamped at zero.** Clamped, because a negative safe-to-spend is not "you may spend −$200" — it is a shortfall, and it is reported as one with its date. The floor is the user's own stated buffer, defaulting to zero, and is always shown alongside so the number is never mistaken for a raw balance.
6. **The projection covers operating cash only.** Accounts classified `checking` / `cash_management` by ADR-033 §1's subtype, via the existing `classifyCashTiers`. Savings, CDs, and brokerage are **not** in the running balance: money that is not going to be spent from an account should not mask a shortfall in the account the bills hit. Accounts with no subtype set are **excluded and named** — never silently included, and never silently dropped.
7. **Degradation is explicit and quantified at four levels** (ADR-021's precedent, generalized as ADR-034 §7 did for advice): **no operating account classified** → no projection at all, with a link to set subtypes; **fewer than 3 months of history** → `insufficient_history`, no rate, obligations still shown on the calendar; **zero confirmed obligations** → the projection runs as a pure run-rate and is **labeled as such**, with the detection candidates surfaced as the fix; **thin coverage** (confirmed obligations account for under a stated share of trailing spend) → the number is shown with the coverage percentage next to it. In every case the missing input is **named** and **linked**, per ADR-032's auditability contract.
8. **Every figure carries its inputs.** The projection surface reuses the `InputUsed` shape from [advice/types.ts](../../src/lib/advice/types.ts) rather than inventing a parallel audit trail — same contract, same renderer, same reason: a user who disagrees must be able to find the wrong *input*, not conclude the feature is untrustworthy.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The projection is arithmetic a user can follow line by line, and every line is on screen.
- ✅ Reuses `observedNetIncome`, `classifyCashTiers`, `spendByMonth`, and `InputUsed` — four shipped, tested components.
- ✅ Improves automatically as obligations are confirmed, with the improvement visible as a rising coverage figure.
- ⚠️ A flat daily discretionary rate does not know that spending clusters on weekends and after payday. Stated as a documented limitation (docs/CASHFLOW.md); a day-of-week model is deferred, not hidden.
- ⚠️ Variable-amount obligations (a utility bill) are projected at their trailing median and will be wrong by the variance. `amount_mode='trailing_median'` narrows this; it does not close it.
- ⚠️ The residual-rate subtraction is only as exact as occurrence matching. A systematically unmatched obligation double-counts, which is why the coverage figure is displayed rather than buried.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Total-spend run-rate plus dated obligations, no residual | The double-count. Silently understates safe-to-spend, and it fails in the direction that makes the whole feature untrustworthy. |
| Obligations only, no discretionary rate | Ignores groceries and gas — the projection would be wildly optimistic, which is the more dangerous direction of the two. |
| Project gross income into checking | Overstates every inflow by withholding, 401k, and premiums. ADR-034 §1 already established observed-net as the honest figure. |
| Include savings and brokerage in the running balance | Masks a checking shortfall behind money that is not going to be spent from checking. The whole point is to surface the shortfall. |
| Per-category daily rates instead of one residual | More machinery, no better answer at this horizon, and more ways to double-count. Revisit only if the flat rate is measurably wrong in real use. |
| Monte Carlo / confidence bands | ADR-022 stands (see also ADR-036 §7). |
| A 12-month horizon by default | Beyond ~90 days the residual rate dominates and the line stops being information. The default should be the window a family can act in. |

It submits through its own action so saving either form never blanks the other's columns on the singleton row. **One cost is named rather than hidden**: while a household has no confirmed obligations the dashboard now runs candidate detection on every load, because the run-rate-only rung quotes the waiting count — left unoptimized deliberately (CLAUDE.md §Performance: measure first), since confirming one bill turns it off permanently and a skip flag would make the card's copy diverge from the page's. `/plan/cashflow` is in the nav manifest under Planning, ahead of the review page; longest-prefix resolution means `/plan/cashflow/review` still resolves to its own entry, and the coverage guard in `resolve.test.ts` is what forced the entry to exist. `tailwind-patterns`, `react-ui-patterns` and `data-storytelling` were consulted and contributed nothing the shipped idioms did not already carry — the `/plan` page's notice-above-the-figure ordering, `DashboardCard.Empty`, and the `/settings/household` form idiom were the real references, per SKILLS.md §Maintenance. `fixing-accessibility` was applied to the new form: labelled inputs, `aria-describedby` help text, `role="alert"` on failure, and `aria-labelledby` on every section of the page. Full suite green: **1762 tests / 146 files** (up from 1730/145), `pnpm check` clean apart from the one pre-existing nursery `useSortedClasses` warning on an unrelated account-deletion component, `pnpm build` compiles, typechecks and generates all **25** pages before the known Windows standalone-symlink EPERM.

## Open Items (deferred — record, don't build)

- **Day-of-week / post-payday spend clustering** in the residual rate. The flat daily rate is a documented limitation (ADR-038 consequence 1). Revisit only if the projection is measurably wrong in real use — `performance-profiling`'s "measure, don't guess" applied to a model rather than to code.
- **Variable-amount obligation forecasting** (a utility bill following a seasonal curve). `amount_mode='trailing_median'` narrows the error; a seasonal model is speculation until a real utility series says otherwise.
- **Sinking funds / planned one-off expenses** — [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 12, and already an open item in [PLAN15.md](PLAN15.md). The obligations model here may subsume the recurring half; the dated-one-off half remains its own round.
- **Scenario overlay on the projection** — Round 13. *"What does this line look like with a $650 car payment?"* is a natural extension of `CashFlowEvent`, and it is deliberately not in this phase.
- **Obligation → budget reconciliation** — a confirmed obligation implies a floor under its category's budget. Real, and cleanly separable; do not entangle it with Batch 5.
- **Insurance policies as first-class records** with renewal dates feeding the calendar — [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) open question 4, unanswered.
- **Multi-account projection** (a household running bills out of two checking accounts, transferring between them). Currently all operating accounts are summed into one running balance, which hides a shortfall in one of them. Note this as a stated assumption; revisit if the household actually operates that way.
- **Plaid-provided recurring streams** (`/transactions/recurring/get`). Deliberately not used: local detection over data we already hold has zero egress and no per-Item cost, and ADR-033 §6's manual-first posture applies identically. Revisit only with a §Privacy-clean justification.
- **Notifying safe-to-spend by email** — permanently blocked by ADR-015's content-free email. A shortfall alert may say *that* one exists; it may never carry the number.
