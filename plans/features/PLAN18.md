# PLAN — Phase 18 (Sinking funds: the annual, semiannual and irregular costs a monthly budget structurally cannot represent, the accrual math that sizes them, and one honest sentence about whether the household is holding enough)

> Phase 16 ([PLAN16.md](PLAN16.md)) added the **calendar** — what lands, when, and what that leaves. Phase 17 ([PLAN17.md](PLAN17.md)) added the **explanation** — whether the household earned the change or the market did. Round 12 ([IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md)) asks the question that sits between them: ***"Car insurance is $1,340 in March. I know it, and it still hurts every year."***
>
> **The diagnosis is structural, not motivational.** Budgets are monthly. Families do not fail on monthly — they fail on **annual, semiannual, and irregular**: insurance, property tax, tuition, Christmas, the vet, the HVAC that was always going to die. A monthly budget cannot represent a cost that arrives once a year, so when it arrives it reads as a **blown month** instead of a **known cost**. The household did nothing wrong and the app tells them they did.
>
> The deliverable is one number and one sentence: ***"You should be holding $2,180 against the four known costs in the next 12 months. Your reserve above your emergency-fund target is $600."***
>
> Phase 18 lands in nine batches: (0) **scope gate + ADRs**; (1) **the `planned_expenses` table** — the one-off dated cost, and the only new schema in the phase; (2) **the accrual math** — pure, test-first, one division; (3) **the known-future-cost stream** — unifying planned expenses with non-monthly obligation occurrences, and the cadence threshold that is this phase's double-count trap; (4) **the requirement and the availability** — pure; (5) **the advice rule** — one `RuleId` in the shipped engine; (6) **the loader and `/plan/sinking`** — the page, the entry UI, the server actions, the nav item; (7) **the projection integration** — a fourth `CashFlowEvent` member, so the calendar stops lying about the low-water mark; (8) **tests, privacy pass, docs, E2E, perf**.
>
> **Hard boundary ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Domain Scope) — read before writing code, and read it twice on this phase specifically.** A sinking fund is one short step from an envelope system, and envelope budgeting is **named in §Domain Scope as out** and reaffirmed by [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 12's own boundary clause: *"No money is partitioned, reserved, or moved between virtual buckets. The app computes a number and shows it against reality."* Concretely, this phase does **NOT** add: a `funded_amount` column, a `funded` status, a per-expense progress bar backed by stored contributions, a designated funding account, a transfer or an allocation of any kind, a "move $180 into this bucket" action, or any surface that shows a real balance split into virtual parts. It also does **NOT** add: **user-authored goals with funding rules and per-goal projections** (ADR-022 stands, and PLAN15's Open Item names this exact hazard — a `planned_expense` is a **dated bill you already know about**, not a goal you are working toward); **double-entry accounting or a general ledger**; **tax math**; **probabilistic projection**; **any AI or LLM over financial data** (§Privacy hard-no — every figure here is arithmetic with a stated formula). Lifting sinking funds into scope is justified exactly as ADR-018 lifted budgets, ADR-030 lifted income, ADR-032 lifted guidance, ADR-036 lifted timing and ADR-039 lifted attribution: it is the **owner's explicit ask** in [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 12, recorded in [FEATURE.md](FEATURE.md) under P18-00.
>
> **The line between "sinking fund" and "envelope", stated once so every task can be checked against it.** An envelope system **partitions a real balance** and then constrains spending against the partitions; its state lives in the database and diverges from the bank. A sinking fund as built here **computes a requirement** — a function of dated costs and today's date — and **displays it beside an unpartitioned real balance**. Nothing is reserved, nothing is moved, and if the user deletes every planned expense the money is exactly where it was. **The test of whether a task has crossed the line is whether it makes the app store a number that says where money "is" other than the account it is actually in.** If it does, the design is wrong.
>
> **Privacy ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Privacy & Data Sovereignty) — binding, and the egress budget is again literally zero.** Nothing under `src/lib/sinking/` makes or transitively imports an outbound HTTP call on any code path. There is no insurance-price lookup, no "typical cost of a new roof" service, no benchmark feed, and no enrichment of any kind. This phase **does create new PII**, which the last two did not: `planned_expenses.label` and `.notes` are free text a household writes about itself — *"Emma's braces"*, *"Mom's care-home deposit"* — and they are among the most sensitive strings the app will ever hold. They therefore: never appear in a log line; never appear in the content-free notification email (ADR-015 — and this phase ships **no alert type** at all, so the question does not arise, recorded in P18-00 as a decision rather than an oversight); never leave local Postgres; and are **not** added to the search index in this phase (a decision, recorded — see Open Items). `pg_dump` covers the table automatically under ADR-014's encrypted-backup path with no change required. P18-33's privacy regression test enforces all of it.
>
> **Money & Numeric Precision ([CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) §Money — CRITICAL).** Accrual is division, which is where money math goes wrong. Every value is a `numeric` string combined only through [Money](../../src/lib/money/money.ts)/`decimal.js`; **never** native `+ - * /`. ADR-044 exists specifically because the literal formula in [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) (*"required balance today = elapsed months × the monthly rate"*) **rounds twice and drifts**, and the phase computes it with **one division** instead. `Number()` appears nowhere in `src/lib/sinking/` and only at the display edge inside a client island.
>
> **Date discipline.** All dates are UTC-midnight, inclusive `YYYY-MM-DD` strings. Month arithmetic goes through the **one** `monthsBetween` in [schedule/occurrences.ts](../../src/lib/schedule/occurrences.ts) — the copy P17-07 consolidated — and occurrence expansion through the **one** `occurrenceDatesBetween` (ADR-036 §5). This phase adds **no** date helper of its own; if a task appears to need one, that is the signal that an existing one is being reimplemented.

## What already exists (do not rebuild — extend these)

Inventory before writing code. Phase 18's genuinely new surface is **one table, four pure modules, one rule and one page**; the rest is composition over three shipped phases.

- **The recurring half of the problem is already solved.** [scheduled-obligations.ts](../../src/db/schema/scheduled-obligations.ts) carries `cadence ∈ OBLIGATION_CADENCES`, which **already includes `quarterly`, `semiannual` and `annual`** — precisely because ADR-037 §3 anticipated *"insurance premiums land semiannually, estimated taxes quarterly, property tax and domain renewals annually."* **The $1,340 March car-insurance bill in Round 12's own opening line is already representable today**, as an `annual` obligation. What is missing is not a way to record it; it is the **accrual arithmetic** that turns it into a monthly set-aside and a required balance. Read that sentence twice before writing a schema: it is the single most important scoping fact in the phase, and it is why ADR-043 makes `planned_expenses` a **narrow, one-off-only** table rather than a second bill model.

Future occurrences of a non-monthly obligation are **already dated rows in the database**; the cost stream reads them, it does not re-expand them.
- **Occurrence expansion and month math** — [schedule/occurrences.ts](../../src/lib/schedule/occurrences.ts): `occurrenceDatesBetween`, `parseIsoDate`, and the **one** `monthsBetween` (P17-07's consolidation, enforced by [advice/privacy-egress.test.ts](../../src/lib/advice/privacy-egress.test.ts)). Batch 7 adds a **fourth union member**, which is a change to a shipped exhaustive `switch` and is treated as such.
- **The advice engine** — [advice/engine.ts](../../src/lib/advice/engine.ts) (`runAdvice`, history gating, dismissal, sorting), [advice/types.ts](../../src/lib/advice/types.ts) (`Rule`, `RuleId`, `Recommendation`, `InputUsed`, `Confidence`, `Severity`), [advice/snapshot.ts](../../src/lib/advice/snapshot.ts) (`loadFinancialSnapshot`, `AdviceInput`). - **Zod boundary schemas** — [schemas/cashflow.ts](../../src/lib/schemas/cashflow.ts) is the closest precedent for the new `schemas/planned-expense.ts`: money as a string with a regex, dates as strict ISO, cross-field refinements mirroring the table's CHECKs.

Batch 6 follows this shape rather than inventing one.
- **Nav** — [nav/manifest.ts](../../src/lib/nav/manifest.ts): the Planning section already carries Overview / Net worth change / Budgets / Review queue / Guidance / Cash flow / Recurring bills. One item is **added there**, not hand-wired (ADR-035).
- **Naming hazard, and it is a bad one.** The app already has a `/plan` route tree meaning *guidance*, a `waterfall-ladder.tsx` meaning *the savings-priority ladder*, `budgets` meaning *monthly category targets*, and `reserve` meaning *the emergency-fund cash tier*. "Fund" and "plan" are both overloaded already. The new module is `src/lib/sinking/`, the new table is `planned_expenses`, the new route is `/plan/sinking`, and the new rule id is `sinking_fund`. **Do not name anything in this phase `goal`, `bucket`, `envelope`, `allocation`, or `reserve`** — the first four describe features that are out of scope and the fifth already means something else.

### ADR-043 — `planned_expenses` is a narrow one-off table: the recurring half stays in `scheduled_obligations`, and a sinking fund reserves nothing

**Status**: proposed — P18-00 is the gate

**Context**: [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 12.1 asks for *"a named, dated, amount-bearing future cost"* and notes in the same breath that *"Round 10's obligations model may subsume the recurring half of it."* Working that through against the shipped schema: it already does. `OBLIGATION_CADENCES` carries `quarterly | semiannual | annual` (ADR-037 §3), and the round's own example — annual car insurance — is an `annual` obligation today. What is genuinely unrepresentable is the cost that happens **once**: a roof, a wedding, a deposit, this December's Christmas. That is one narrow gap, and a table sized to it is very different from a table sized to "future costs" in general.

**Decision**:

1. **`planned_expenses` holds one-off dated costs only.** No cadence column, no recurrence, no occurrence table, no materialization job. A cost that repeats is a `scheduled_obligation`, which already expands, materializes, matches and alerts. **Two tables, one for each shape, with no overlap in what they can express.**
2. **Rejected: adding a `one_off` member to `OBLIGATION_CADENCES`.** It would push a non-repeating case through `occurrenceDatesBetween` (which enumerates from an anchor in *both* directions), the `project-cashflow-daily` materializer, the miss sweep, the matcher, and two alert types — every one of which is written around the assumption that another occurrence follows. The cadence vocabulary is also shared with income by a compile-time assertion ([schedule/cadence.ts](../../src/lib/schedule/cadence.ts)); a member meaning "never again" would be a lie in both vocabularies.
3. **Nothing is reserved, partitioned, or moved — and the schema is what enforces it.** There is **no `funded_amount`, no `funded` status, no `funding_account_id`, and no contributions child table.** The absence is the design, not an omission to be filled in later: the moment a column records where money "is" other than the account it is actually in, this is an envelope system and §Domain Scope forbids it. `status ∈ {planned, paid, cancelled}` describes **the cost**, never the funding.
4. **No `account_id` either, and the reason is the same.** A planned expense is a household-level future cost. Naming an account would imply an earmark the app does not enforce and cannot honour, and the cash-flow projection already runs against the aggregated operating-cash pool (ADR-038 §6), so there is nowhere for a per-account figure to go.
5. **Manual entry only; nothing is detected.** There is no periodicity in a one-off event, so there is nothing for a detector to find — the confirm-never-infer posture of ADR-036 §2 holds here trivially rather than by effort.
6. **`due_date` may become past.** Entry validates future-or-today at the Zod boundary, but the column is unconstrained relative to `now`, because time passes and a row that was valid yesterday must not become illegal today. A past-due `planned` row is surfaced as past-due with `Mark paid` / `Cancel` actions. **No matcher is built** — pairing a one-off cost to a transaction is a whole feature (P16-22 is the evidence) for a case the user can resolve with one click.
7. **`status` is text + CHECK, not `pgEnum`** — ADR-037 §7's standing posture, for the same reason.
8. **This is still not an ADR-022 goal.** PLAN15's Open Item put it plainly and it is carried here verbatim: no projection, no progress tracking, no priority ranking, no funding rules. A planned expense is **a bill you already know about**, and the difference from a goal is that you did not choose to have it.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The gap Round 12 names is closed with **one narrow table and one migration**, and the recurring case reuses three phases of shipped machinery instead of a parallel one.
- ✅ The envelope boundary is enforced by the **absence of columns**, which is the only enforcement that survives a future contributor who has not read this ADR.
- ✅ Deleting every planned expense leaves the household's money exactly where it was, provably, because nothing was ever moved.
- ⚠️ **A cost can be double-entered** — once as a planned expense and once as an annual obligation — and the total would count it twice. Not preventable by structure (the two rows are legitimately different records), so it is handled by disclosure: ADR-045 §4's entry-time collision warning plus the always-itemized cost list.
- ⚠️ **A recurring-but-irregular cost with no fixed cadence** (a vet bill; "the car needs something twice a year but not on a schedule") fits neither table cleanly. The honest answer today is a series of one-off planned expenses. Recorded as an Open Item, not solved by a `cadence: 'irregular'` that would mean "we do not know", which is not a schedule.
- ⚠️ New PII: `label` and `notes` are free text about the household. Handled under §Privacy above.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Add `one_off` to `OBLIGATION_CADENCES` and reuse `scheduled_obligations` | Pushes a non-repeating case through an expander, a materializer, a matcher, a miss sweep and two alert types all written around "another one follows". Also corrupts a vocabulary shared with income by compile-time assertion. |
| A general `future_costs` table replacing both | Rewrites three shipped phases to close one narrow gap, and would need a cadence column anyway — arriving back at `scheduled_obligations` with a different name and no history. |
| Add `funded_amount` / a contributions child table | This is the envelope system §Domain Scope forbids and Round 12's own boundary clause excludes. Also creates a second source of truth that diverges from the bank silently. |
| Add `funding_account_id` | Implies an earmark the app cannot enforce; the projection runs on aggregated operating cash, so the column would have no consumer. |
| Reuse `category_budgets` with a `target_date` | Budgets are **monthly recurring targets** (ADR-018). A dated one-off cost is a different noun, and overloading the table would make every budget query ask "is this one of the real ones?". |
| Build a transaction matcher for planned expenses | A whole feature (P16-22's size is the evidence) for a case one click resolves. Revisit only if manual `Mark paid` actually chafes. |

### ADR-044 — The accrual identity: straight-line over whole months from a stated funding start, computed with **one** division, clamped to `[0, amount]`

**Status**: proposed — P18-00 is the gate

**Context**: [IDEAS3.md](IDEAS3.md) Round 12.2 states the math as *"required monthly set-aside = amount ÷ months to due; required balance today = elapsed months × that rate."* That is the right model and the wrong implementation. Taken literally it **rounds twice**: once when the rate is computed to cents, again when the rate is multiplied back. On $1,340 over 7 months the rate is $191.43 and seven of them is $1,340.01 — a cent the household never owed. Over four costs and twelve months the drift is small but it is *systematic*, and it lands on a figure whose entire credibility is that it reconciles.

**Decision**:

1. **`requiredToday` is computed with one division and is the authoritative figure**: `amount × elapsedMonths ÷ totalMonths`, as `m(amount).mul(elapsed).div(total)`. One rounding, monotone in `elapsed`, and **exactly `amount` when `elapsed === total`** — the property that matters, because on the day the bill is due the requirement must equal the bill.
2. **`perMonth` is a separate display figure**, computed as `m(amount).div(total)`. This is `div`, not `divFloor`, and [money.ts](../../src/lib/money/money.ts)'s own header says why: *"Use `div` to SPLIT a known total … use `divFloor` for a rate."* A set-aside is a fair split of a known total, and — unlike ADR-038 §2's projection rate — the dangerous direction here is **under**-collecting, which `divFloor` would guarantee. `perMonth × elapsed` may differ from `requiredToday` by a cent or two; **`requiredToday` wins**, and the module header says so rather than leaving a future reader to discover it from a failing test.
3. **`totalMonths = max(1, monthsBetween(fundingStart, dueDate))`**, via the one shipped `monthsBetween`. The floor at 1 is not defensive coding: without it a cost due in ten days divides by zero, and the honest answer for a cost due inside a month is *"you need all of it now"* — which is exactly what `totalMonths = 1` produces.
4. **`fundingStart` is explicit, stored, and defaulted rather than inferred.** `accrual_start_date` on the row, defaulting at entry to today. Deriving it from `created_at` would make the required balance jump backwards when an unrelated field is edited; deriving it from `due_date − 12 months` would tell a household that entered a cost yesterday that they are already $900 behind — the single most demoralizing possible first impression, and a false one.
5. **`elapsedMonths = clamp(monthsBetween(fundingStart, today), 0, totalMonths)`.** A funding start in the future yields `0` and `requiredToday = "0.00"`; a due date in the past yields `totalMonths` and `requiredToday = amount`. Both clamps are stated, not incidental.
6. **Whole months, not days.** `monthsBetween` floors, so the requirement steps up on the monthly anniversary rather than creeping daily. Deliberate: a household sets money aside **per paycheck or per month**, and a figure that moves every morning invites the user to check whether it moved rather than whether they are on track. Stated as a design choice so nobody "fixes" it to daily proration.
7. **A `paid` or `cancelled` cost contributes nothing** — `requiredToday = "0.00"`, excluded from every total, still visible in the list with its status. Deactivate, do not delete (§Database).
8. **Pure and I/O-free.** `accrue(cost, today)` takes values and returns values. Every DB read lives in the one loader.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The requirement equals the bill on the due date, **exactly, as a string** — an assertion in the invariant suite, not an aspiration.
- ✅ `requiredToday` is monotone non-decreasing in `today` for a fixed cost, a property a household will notice if it is ever violated, and which P18-08 asserts across a full month-by-month walk.
- ✅ One division means the drift IDEAS3's literal formula carries simply does not exist, rather than being bounded.
- ⚠️ **The refinement of Round 12's stated formula is deliberate and is recorded as such**, so a future reader comparing the round to the code finds the reason instead of a discrepancy.
- ⚠️ Straight-line only. A household that would rather front-load (a bonus in March) gets no support for that, and should not: a funding *schedule* is a funding rule, and funding rules are ADR-022 goals.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Implement IDEAS3's formula literally (`perMonth × elapsed`) | Rounds twice; drifts systematically; can exceed the cost itself on the due date. |
| `divFloor` for `perMonth` | Guarantees under-collection — the dangerous direction for a set-aside, and the mirror image of ADR-038 §2's reasoning, not a contradiction of it. |
| Daily proration | Technically smoother, practically worse: households fund monthly, and a number that moves every morning trains the user to watch the number instead of the plan. |
| Infer `fundingStart` from `due_date − 12 months` | Tells a household that just recorded a cost that it is already thousands behind. False, and demoralizing in the one place the feature is trying to help. |
| Infer `fundingStart` from `created_at` | The required balance jumps backwards on an unrelated edit. A displayed figure must not move for reasons the user cannot see. |

### ADR-045 — The requirement spans **both** sources, and obligations of monthly-or-shorter cadence are excluded by construction

**Status**: proposed — P18-00 is the gate

**Context**: This is Phase 18's ADR-038 §2 — the one place a plausible-looking design silently produces a wrong number, and it is wrong in the direction that makes the app alarmist. Round 12's own example sentence — *"$2,180 against the four known costs in the next 12 months"* — quietly requires the answer to span both sources: some of those four costs are one-off planned expenses, and some (the car insurance the round opens with) are annual obligations. A requirement computed from `planned_expenses` alone would omit the very bill that motivated the round.

**Decision**:

1. **The cost stream is the union of two sources**: (a) `planned_expenses` with `status='planned'` and `due_date` inside the horizon; (b) `obligation_occurrences` with `status='scheduled'` inside the horizon whose parent obligation is `active`, `direction='outflow'`, and **whose cadence is longer than monthly**. Each yields a `KnownFutureCost { source, sourceId, label, amount, dueDate, fundingStart }`, and everything downstream sees one shape.
2. **Obligations of cadence `weekly | biweekly | semimonthly | monthly` are excluded, and this is the trap.** A monthly bill is paid out of the month's income. It is **already inside** `essentials.monthly`, which sizes the emergency-fund target the requirement is measured against (ADR-046), **and** inside the trailing observed spend behind the cash-flow residual rate (ADR-038 §2). Requiring a sinking reserve for it would demand the household hold a **second copy** of money it already budgets monthly — on a household with $2,700/month of monthly obligations a naive 12-month stream would demand roughly $16,000 of reserve against bills that are simply paid as they arrive, and the rule would tell a perfectly solvent family it is catastrophically behind. The eligible set is an **exported named constant** (`SINKING_ELIGIBLE_CADENCES`) with the rationale in a comment, derived from `OBLIGATION_CADENCES` by an **exhaustive mapping** so that adding a cadence without deciding its eligibility **fails the build** rather than silently defaulting.
3. **`fundingStart` for an obligation-sourced cost is the previous occurrence's due date**, or the horizon start when there is none — i.e. you accrue for next March's insurance across the twelve months since last March's. This is the whole reason annual costs are fundable at all, and it falls out of the cadence rather than needing a stored column.
4. **Duplicates are disclosed, never merged.** A cost entered both as a planned expense and as an obligation is two legitimate rows, and no structural rule distinguishes it from two genuinely different costs on the same day. Matching on label similarity is exactly the brittle string guess ADR-040 §2 rejected. Instead: (a) **every surface that shows the total also itemizes the costs behind it** — name, source, date, amount — so a duplicate is visible on the same screen as the number it inflates; and (b) the planned-expense entry form **warns at preview time** when the new row collides with an existing eligible occurrence on `(amount within tolerance, due_date within ±N days)`, naming the obligation and linking to it. **The warning never blocks and never merges** — ADR-036 §2's preview-and-confirm posture applied literally.
5. **The horizon is 12 months**, an exported constant, because Round 12's own sentence says *"in the next 12 months"* and because a longer horizon accrues for costs the household cannot yet size.
6. **Inflows are excluded.** An obligation with `direction='inflow'` is money arriving; there is nothing to set aside for it.
7. **The stream is assembled by the loader and consumed pure.** `buildCostStream(plannedRows, occurrenceRows, obligations, today, horizonMonths)` is a pure function over already-fetched rows.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ The requirement answers the question Round 12 actually asked, including the annual insurance bill in its opening line.
- ✅ The double-count that would have made the feature unusable is prevented **by an exhaustive compile-checked mapping**, not by a filter someone can quietly widen.
- ✅ Because obligation-sourced costs come from **already-materialized occurrence rows**, the stream needs no new expansion, no new job, and no second interpretation of a schedule.
- ⚠️ **The horizon is a hard edge.** A cost 13 months out contributes nothing today and appears abruptly next month at a full month's accrual. Accepted: the alternative accrues for costs the household cannot size. Disclosed on the page as "costs due in the next 12 months".
- ⚠️ **Occurrence coverage bounds the stream.** `project-cashflow-daily` materializes to roughly `today + projection horizon + MATERIALIZATION_LEAD_DAYS`, which is far short of 12 months, so **annual and semiannual occurrences beyond the materialized window will be missing from the stream** unless the materializer's lead is extended for eligible cadences. **Verified at P18-00, and it is certain rather than likely**: both call sites — [project-cashflow-daily.ts](../../src/worker/jobs/project-cashflow-daily.ts) and `materializeThroughHorizon` in [review/actions.ts](../../src/app/plan/cashflow/review/actions.ts) — materialize through `today + projectionHorizonDays + MATERIALIZATION_LEAD_DAYS`, about **90 days at the defaults (60 + 30)**. **P18-11 must still re-verify this against the shipped job before Batch 4 depends on it** — this is the single most likely way this phase ships a quietly-too-small number, and it is called out here rather than discovered in review. If the lead must be extended, that is a change to a shipped repeatable job and gets `cron-doctor` plus its own note.
- ⚠️ A `trailing_median` obligation contributes its current estimate, which will move. Stated on the page, the same way ADR-038 already states it for the projection.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Read `planned_expenses` only | Omits the annual car-insurance bill that opens Round 12. The feature would not answer its own motivating example. |
| Include every obligation cadence | Demands a second copy of money already budgeted monthly; tells a solvent household it is ~$16k behind. The defining error of this phase. |
| Auto-merge duplicates by label similarity | The brittle string guess ADR-040 §2 rejected. Silently dropping a cost from a total is worse than showing it twice next to its own name. |
| Block a colliding entry | Two costs really can fall on one day for one amount. Blocking makes a real state inexpressible; warning does not. |
| Re-expand obligation schedules instead of reading occurrences | A second interpretation of a schedule the materializer already interprets. Two answers to "when is this due" is how they drift. |
| An unbounded horizon | Accrues today for a roof in 2034 the household cannot size, and makes the number never stop growing. |

### ADR-046 — Availability is the reserve tier **above** the emergency-fund target, and the rule degrades rather than reassures

**Status**: proposed — P18-00 is the gate

**Context**: A requirement with nothing to compare it to is trivia. Round 12's own sentence names the comparison: *"your reserve above emergency target is $600."* Choosing which balance that is decides whether the rule is useful or actively misleading.

**Decision**:

1. **`available = max(0, cash.reserve.total − emergencyFundTarget)`**, where `emergencyFundTarget = reserveMonthsFor(input).months × essentials.monthly` — **the same `reserveMonthsFor` the emergency-fund rule, the waterfall rung and the cash-drag rule already call**. Fourth caller, not second implementation.
2. **Reserve tier only.** Not operating cash, which is committed to this month's bills — the argument [emergency-fund.ts](../../src/lib/advice/rules/emergency-fund.ts) already makes, applying unchanged. Not medium-term/CDs, which carry a break penalty. Counting either would report a fund the household cannot actually spend when the bill lands, which is the one circumstance the number exists for.
3. **Emergency fund first, and the rule says so rather than the waterfall being re-ordered.** You do not fund next March's insurance before you have a job-loss reserve. But re-ordering the shipped ADR-034 §4 ladder would amend a locked ADR and change a shipped surface as a side effect of a new feature — the thing ADR-039 §2 declined to do with the quantity wart. Instead the rule **caps its own severity at `info` while the emergency-fund target is unmet** and states the ordering in its `rationale`; when the reserve is funded and the sinking requirement is not, it emits `warning`. Whether `sinking_fund` becomes a formal eighth rung is an Open Item.
4. **`unknown_input` when `essentials.monthly` is `null`** — no baseline, no target, no available figure. Names the missing input and links to it (ADR-032 §2), exactly like the emergency-fund rule.
5. **`unknown_input`, not `ok`, when the cost stream is empty.** The sharpest judgement call in the rule, made deliberately. The app cannot distinguish *"this household has no large irregular costs"* from *"this household has not recorded them yet"*, and the second is overwhelmingly more likely on any install. Emitting `ok` would tell a family with an unrecorded $1,340 insurance bill that they are fine — the exact failure `RungStatus`'s *"never report `done` on absent data"* rule exists to prevent. The rule instead names the missing input and links to `/plan/sinking`.
6. **`minMonthsHistory: 0`.** The rule reads recorded costs and recorded balances, not transaction history — the same argument that puts the APR-based rules at zero.
7. **`confidence`** follows the shipped `baselineConfidence` helper on the essential baseline, since the target it derives from is only as good as that baseline.
8. **Nothing is stored.** The requirement, the availability and the gap are computed on read (ADR-032 §3, ADR-036 §4, ADR-039 §3). There is no `sinking_fund_state` table and adding one would be a mistake for the same reason: it goes stale the moment a cost is edited.
9. **ADR-048 §5 binds the cash-flow surface** *(added at P18-00 — ADR-048 was locked after this plan was drafted)*. `/plan/cashflow` states no emergency-fund target, so the one sentence Phase 18 adds there (P18-27) links to `/plan/sinking` and does **not** restate `available` or the target.

**Consequences**:
- ✅ One `RuleId` in the shipped engine, with dismissal, snooze, sorting and the audit trail all working for free.
- ✅ The number `/plan/sinking` shows and the number the guidance card shows **cannot disagree**, because both call the same two pure functions over the same `AdviceInput`.
- ✅ The rule is honest on a blank install rather than reassuring, which is the property that makes every other rule in the engine trustworthy.
- ⚠️ **`/plan/sinking` pays for a full `loadFinancialSnapshot`**, because that is what guarantees the previous point. Measured in P18-35; if it is slow, the fix is a narrower loader that *composes the same pure functions*, never a second computation of the same figures.
- ⚠️ A household whose reserve is short will see `info` on a real gap. Accepted and stated: the emergency fund genuinely is the more urgent problem, and the rationale says so in words.

**Rejected alternatives**:

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Measure against total cash or `cash.liquid` | Counts this month's bill money and CDs with break penalties as available. Reports a fund that cannot be spent when the bill lands. |
| Measure against reserve without subtracting the emergency target | Double-counts the emergency fund as sinking money, and would call a household funded that has one pot doing two jobs. |
| Re-order the ADR-034 §4 waterfall to insert a sinking rung | Amends a locked ADR and changes a shipped surface as a side effect of a new feature. Recorded as an Open Item instead. |
| Emit `ok` on an empty cost stream | Tells a family with an unrecorded $1,340 bill that they are fine. The precise failure `RungStatus`'s "never `done` on absent data" rule exists to prevent. |
| Store the computed requirement nightly | Stale the moment a cost is edited, and the horizon is relative to today, so a stored grain answers no question correctly. |
| A dedicated `/plan/sinking` loader that recomputes the target | Two answers to "what is your emergency-fund target" on two pages. The whole reason `reserveMonthsFor` is exported. |

## Acceptance criteria

Phase 18 is done when all of the following hold:

1. **The scope gate is honoured.** ADR-043 → ADR-046 are in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md) §11, inserted into the reserved gap between ADR-042 and ADR-047, the new rows pass the `awk -F'|'` field count, no accepted ADR was edited, and [FEATURE.md](FEATURE.md) carries the Round 12 → Phase 18 entry.
2. **Nothing is reserved.** `planned_expenses` has no `funded_amount`, no `funded` status, no `funding_account_id`, and no contributions table; no surface shows a real balance split into virtual parts; deleting every planned expense leaves every balance unchanged.
3. **The accrual identity holds exactly.** `requiredToday === amount` **as a string** on the due date; monotone non-decreasing across a day-by-day walk; never exceeding `amount`; and the invariant suite **fails** on each of the three deliberately broken implementations P18-08 names.
4. **The double-count is prevented structurally.** No `monthly`-or-shorter obligation appears in the cost stream; `isSinkingEligible` is an exhaustive record such that adding a cadence without a verdict fails `tsc`; a test asserts the exclusion by name.
5. **Occurrence coverage is proven, not assumed.** An annual obligation due 11 months out appears in the stream (P18-11), by whichever of the two routes was chosen, with the choice recorded.
6. **The rule is honest on a blank install.** An empty cost stream yields `unknown_input`, **not** `ok`; a null essential baseline yields `unknown_input`; both name the missing input and link to where it is fixed.
7. **One number, one implementation.** The requirement string shown on `/plan/sinking` and the one in the `sinking_fund` recommendation are identical for a fixture (P18-32); `reserveMonthsFor` has exactly four callers (emergency-fund, waterfall, cash-drag, sinking-fund) and no reimplementation; `monthsBetween` still has exactly one implementation under `src/lib/` (the private, now-relative `monthsBetween(startIso)` in `src/app/accounts/[id]/page.tsx` pre-dates this phase and is out of its scope — the check is that Phase 18 adds none).
8. **The projection tells the truth.** A planned expense inside the horizon moves the low-water mark and safe-to-spend; `residualDailyRate` and `coverage` are unchanged by it (P18-30); a past-due, `paid` or `cancelled` cost emits no event.
9. **Money discipline.** No `Number()` and no native arithmetic on a money value anywhere under `src/lib/sinking/`; every money column is `numeric(18,2)` carried as a string.
10. **Privacy.** P18-33 green: zero egress on every transitive path; no `console.*`; `label`/`notes` unreachable from the alert-email path and absent from the search index; `pg_dump` coverage confirmed with no backup-path change.
11. **Green and documented.** `pnpm check` and `pnpm test` clean; the Playwright flow passes; `docs/SINKING.md` exists with its "what this is not" section; the nav item is in the manifest and resolves in ⌘K.

## Open Items (deferred — record, don't build)

- **Recurring-but-irregular costs with no fixed cadence** — the vet, the car that needs something twice a year at no predictable interval. Fits neither table cleanly (ADR-043 consequence 2). The honest answer today is a series of one-off planned expenses. **Do not** solve it with a `cadence: 'irregular'`, which would mean "we do not know" and is not a schedule. Revisit only if this shape shows up repeatedly in real use.
- **A transaction matcher for planned expenses** — deliberately not built (ADR-043 §6); `Mark paid` is one click. Revisit only if that actually chafes, and then reuse [cashflow/match.ts](../../src/lib/cashflow/match.ts) rather than writing a second matcher.
- **`sinking_fund` as a formal eighth waterfall rung** — ADR-046 §3 caps severity and states the ordering in prose instead, because re-ordering the ADR-034 §4 ladder is an amendment to a locked ADR and a change to a shipped surface. If it is ever wanted, it needs that amendment on its own merits, not as a ride-along.
- **Search indexing of planned expenses** — a decision, not an oversight (P18-00), and enforced by P18-33. `label`/`notes` are the most sensitive free text in the app; indexing them is a §Privacy question deserving its own pass, alongside Round 15's bus-file work which raises the same concentration concern.
- **A sinking-fund alert** ("a cost is due in 30 days and you are $600 short") — Round 12 does not ask for one, and it would be a *forward-looking input-health* signal, which is Round 16's job done once across all inputs. Also note ADR-015's content-free constraint: such an alert must never name the cost.
- **Front-loaded or paycheck-aligned funding schedules** — a funding *rule*, which is an ADR-022 goal. Straight-line only (ADR-044 consequence 2).
- **ADR-048's covered-shortfall remainder does not net out the sinking requirement** *(found at P18-00)*. `/plan/cashflow` says a transfer from reserve *"leaves $X in reserve"*, and that $X may be next March's insurance. Folding the requirement in would amend a just-locked ADR as a ride-along, which ADR-046 §3 declines on principle. If it is wanted, it is a small ADR-048 amendment on its own merits, and it must still respect ADR-048 §5 (no target on that page).
- **Multi-currency planned expenses** — the column exists and defaults to `USD`; the totals assume the base currency. Cross-currency accrual would go through `fx_rates` at the due date, which is a real design question nobody has asked yet.
- **A "what changed" view on the requirement** — why the number moved between two visits. Genuinely useful, genuinely Round 16-shaped (input health), and not asked for here.
